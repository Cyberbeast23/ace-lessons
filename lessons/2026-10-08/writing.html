✍️ Writing Week 11: Breaking News! A Volcano Erupts

Yesterday you wrote like a scientist. Today, Ace, you'll write like a news reporter. When a volcano erupts, people need the most important facts fast. Reporters use a special shape called the inverted pyramid: the biggest, most important facts go at the top, and the smaller details come later. You'll learn how to write a strong headline, a lead that answers the 5 Ws + H, and how to use quotations correctly. Then you'll file your own breaking news story!

Part 1: The Lesson

A news report has five parts (the inverted pyramid 🔻)

Readers might stop after one paragraph, so reporters put the most important facts first:

1. Headline: a short, punchy title with a strong present-tense verb
2. Lead: the first sentence, answering who, what, when, where, why, how
3. Key details: the next most important facts and numbers
4. Quote: the exact words of a witness or expert, in quotation marks
5. Background: extra information that helps readers understand (history, science)

Five reporter writing moves

  • A headline that grabs: leave out small words and use a strong verb in the present tense. "Mount St. Helens Erupts, Flattens Forests"
  • A lead that does the heavy lifting: pack the 5 Ws + H into one or two sentences. "A huge eruption blasted the top off Mount St. Helens in Washington State on Sunday morning, after an earthquake shook loose the mountain's north side."
  • Facts, not opinions: a reporter writes "the blast flattened about 600 km² of forest," not "it was the scariest thing ever." Let the people you quote give the feelings.
  • Quotations done right: exact words go inside quotation marks, the comma or period goes inside the closing mark, and you say who spoke: "We could see the ash cloud from our farm," said Ruth Olsen.
  • Past tense, used consistently: the story already happened, so keep your verbs in the past tense (except in the headline).

Useful reporter linking words: according to, earlier that morning, meanwhile, as a result, officials said, by Sunday afternoon.

Model News Report (short version)

Headline + leadKey detailsQuoteBackground

Mount St. Helens Erupts, Flattens Forests
A huge eruption blasted the top off Mount St. Helens in Washington State on the morning of May 18, 1980, after a magnitude 5.1 earthquake shook loose the volcano's north side.
The sideways blast flattened trees across hundreds of square kilometres, and a cloud of ash rose more than 20 km into the sky. Officials said the area had been closed for weeks, which kept many people out of danger.
"It was like the whole sky turned black at noon," said a farmer who lives 150 km to the east.
Scientists had been watching the mountain since March, when small earthquakes began and a bulge started to grow on its north side as magma pushed upward.

Notice: The headline uses a strong present-tense verb, the lead answers the 5 Ws + H, the details use exact numbers, the quote has correct punctuation, and the background comes last. (The eruption facts are real; the farmer's quote is made up for this model.)
