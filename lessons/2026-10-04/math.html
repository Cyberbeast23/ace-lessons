🌉 Math Week 7: Bridges & Structural Engineering Lab

Gear up, Ace! Today you'll use ratios/scale, area, volume, percentages, fractions, mean/median/range, unit rates, BEDMAS, elapsed time, and unit conversions — just like a civil engineer designing decks, cables, and spans for a Canadian bridge.

Part 1: Learn the Moves

The 4-Step Problem Solver (your structural check-up):
  1. Read: What is the question actually asking? Underline the numbers and units.
  2. Plan: Which operations do I need, and in what order?
  3. Solve: Do one step at a time and write each step down.
  4. Check: Is my answer reasonable? Did I answer the question that was asked?

Worked Example 1: Bridge scale model

A museum model of a suspension bridge is built at a scale of 1 : 500. The model span is 3.0 m long. How long is the real span in metres?

Step 1: Real length = model × scale factor = 3.0 × 500 = 1500 m.

Check: 1500 ÷ 500 = 3.0 ✔

Worked Example 2: Fraction of a span

A river crossing is 480 m long. The main deck section is 3/8 of that span. How long is the main deck?

Step 1: 480 ÷ 8 = 60; 60 × 3 = 180 m.

Worked Example 3: Percent of cable load

A cable can safely carry 800 tonnes. It is currently carrying 65% of that load. How many tonnes is it carrying?

Step 1: 65% = 0.65.

Step 2: 0.65 × 800 = 520 tonnes.

Worked Example 4: Mean, median, and range

Five pier heights (metres): 12, 15, 14, 13, and 16. Find the mean, median, and range.

Mean = (12 + 15 + 14 + 13 + 16) ÷ 5 = 70 ÷ 5 = 14 m.

Ordered: 12, 13, 14, 15, 16. Median = 14 m.

Range = 16 − 12 = 4 m.

Worked Example 5: Volume, conversion, and BEDMAS

Convert 4500 mm of beam thickness to metres: 4500 ÷ 1000 = 4.5 m.

A concrete footing is 8 m × 2.5 m × 1.5 m. Volume = 8 × 2.5 × 1.5 = 30 m³.

BEDMAS: (96 − 36) ÷ 12 × 3² + 5 → 60 ÷ 12 = 5; 3² = 9; 5 × 9 = 45; 45 + 5 = 50.
