We use Traditional Chinese characters and Jyutping with tone numbers, the Hong Kong way.

How to read Jyutping (sounds):
  • Letters show the sound. Numbers show the tone (the musical pitch): 1 high, 2 rising, 3 mid, 4 low/falling, 5 low rising, 6 low.
  • Example: 贏 jeng4 (win) is tone 4, low and falling, while 輸 syu1 (lose) is tone 1, high and flat. 第一 dai6 jat1 (first) starts low and level (tone 6), then jumps up to a short, clipped high tone 1 because 一 ends in t.
  • Listening/speaking tip: Say each word out loud three times while looking at the characters. Tap Hear model, then Record and compare!

Sports tips for Ace:
  • 希臘 hei1 laap6 = Greece. Add 人 (jan4, person) to make 希臘人, a Greek person, just like 加拿大人 is a Canadian!
  • 運動員 wan6 dung6 jyun4 = athlete: 運動 = sport, exercise + 員 = member, worker. An athlete is a "sport worker."
  • 贏 jeng4 and 輸 syu1 are opposites: win and lose. 我贏咗！ (ngo5 jeng4 zo2!) = I won! The little word 咗 (zo2) shows it already happened.
  • 獎牌 zoeng2 paai4 = medal: 獎 = prize + 牌 = plate, badge. A gold medal is 金牌 (gam1 paai4); you learned 金 on gold rush day!
  • 加油 gaa1 jau2 literally means "add oil," but Hong Kong fans shout it to mean "Go! You can do it!" Shout it at Ace's next game. 💪
