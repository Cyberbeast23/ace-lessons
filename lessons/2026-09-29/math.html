🧮 Math Week 2: Skeleton Math Lab

Yesterday you trained on heart rates and doses. Today, Ace, you'll think like an orthopaedic doctor: counting bones, tracking fracture healing, reading X-ray wait times, and using fractions, ratios, percentages, BEDMAS, mean/range, and area to solve skeleton problems.

Part 1: Learn the Moves

The 4-Step Problem Solver (still your doctor's check-up):
  1. Read: What is the question actually asking? Underline the numbers and units.
  2. Plan: Which operations do I need, and in what order?
  3. Solve: Do one step at a time and write each step down.
  4. Check: Is my answer reasonable? Did I answer the question that was asked?

Worked Example 1: Multi-step with the skeleton

An adult has 206 bones. Each hand has 27 bones. How many bones are not in the two hands?

Step 1: Two hands → 2 × 27 = 54.

Step 2: 206 − 54 = 152 bones.

Check: 152 + 54 = 206 ✔

Worked Example 2: Fractions of healing time

A wrist fracture needs 8 weeks in a cast. After 3/8 of the healing time, how many weeks are left?

Step 1: Time used = 3/8 × 8 = 3 weeks.

Step 2: Time left = 8 − 3 = 5 weeks (or 5/8 of 8).

Worked Example 3: Percentages and calcium

Ace needs about 1000 mg of calcium a day. He has already had 35%. How many milligrams does he still need?

Step 1: 35% of 1000 = 0.35 × 1000 = 350 mg already eaten.

Step 2: 1000 − 350 = 650 mg still needed. (Or find 65% of 1000.)

Worked Example 4: Mean and range

Five patients waited for an X-ray: 10, 22, 14, 18, and 16 minutes. Find the mean and the range.

Mean = (10 + 22 + 14 + 18 + 16) ÷ 5 = 80 ÷ 5 = 16 minutes.

Range = largest − smallest = 22 − 10 = 12 minutes.

Worked Example 5: Area, perimeter, and BEDMAS

An X-ray film is a rectangle 20 cm by 25 cm.

Area = length × width = 20 × 25 = 500 cm².

Perimeter = 2 × (20 + 25) = 2 × 45 = 90 cm.

BEDMAS reminder: Brackets, Exponents, Division/Multiplication (left→right), Addition/Subtraction (left→right).

(30 − 6) ÷ 4 × 2² + 1 → 24 ÷ 4 = 6; 2² = 4; 6 × 4 = 24; 24 + 1 = 25.
