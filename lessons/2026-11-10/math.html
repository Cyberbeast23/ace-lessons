🎨 Math Week 44: Renaissance Genius: Leonardo's Workshop Math Lab

Ace, about 500 years ago in Italy, a left-handed artist named Leonardo da Vinci filled thousands of notebook pages with paintings, inventions and drawings of the human body. He believed that art and science were the same kind of thinking, and almost everything he made used math: the proportions of a face, the perspective lines that make a flat wall look deep, the scale of a flying-machine model. Today you are an apprentice in Leonardo's workshop, using body proportions as fractions, the perimeter of a famous painting, timeline arithmetic, how area grows when you enlarge a sketch, the golden ratio, sharing a paint mixture in a ratio, area and work rates for a wall painting, reflecting a point across a line (mirror writing!), percent increase, scale models, the mean from a table, angles in a perspective triangle, a two-step equation, GST + PST on art supplies, and the mass of a marble block from its volume and density.

Part 1: Learn the Moves

The 4-Step Problem Solver (your apprentice's checklist):
  1. Read: What is the question actually asking? Underline the numbers and units (m, cm, mm, m², cm², m³, kg, mL, °, $, %).
  2. Plan: Do the units match? Change metres to centimetres or hours to minutes before you answer.
  3. Solve: Do one step at a time and write each step down.
  4. Check: Is my answer reasonable? A painting might be 77 cm tall, but not 77 m!

Worked Example 1: Sharing in a ratio

A ratio of 3 : 5 means 3 parts of one thing for every 5 parts of another, so there are 3 + 5 = 8 parts in total. Find the size of one part first.

Share 40 mL in the ratio 3 : 5. One part = 40 ÷ 8 = 5 mL. So 3 × 5 = 15 mL and 5 × 5 = 25 mL.

Worked Example 2: Enlarging a picture (scale factor and area)

If you multiply every length by a scale factor k, the area is multiplied by k × k, not just k!

A 4 cm × 3 cm sketch enlarged by 2 becomes 8 cm × 6 cm. Old area 12 cm², new area 48 cm², which is 12 × 2 × 2 = 4 times bigger.

Worked Example 3: Reflecting across a line

To reflect a point across a vertical line x = c, find how far the point is from the line, then go the same distance on the other side. The y-coordinate stays the same.

Reflect (5, 3) across x = 2: the point is 5 − 2 = 3 units right of the line, so the image is 3 units left: 2 − 3 = −1. Image = (−1, 3).

Worked Example 4: Percent increase

Percent increase = (new − old) ÷ old × 100. Always divide by the old (starting) value.

From $80 to $100: increase = 20. 20 ÷ 80 = 0.25, so a 25% increase.

Worked Example 5: The mean

The mean (average) = total of all values ÷ how many values there are.

12, 15, 9, 14: total = 50. 50 ÷ 4 = 12.5.

Worked Example 6: Angles in a triangle

The three angles inside any triangle add up to 180°. Subtract the two you know from 180.

Angles 50° and 75°: 180 − 50 − 75 = 55°.

Worked Example 7: Mass from volume and density

Density tells how many kilograms are packed into each cubic metre. Mass = density × volume.

A stone block of 0.5 m³ with a density of 2 000 kg per m³: 2 000 × 0.5 = 1 000 kg.

Worked Example 8: Two taxes at once

In British Columbia many items have 5% GST + 7% PST = 12% tax. Multiply the subtotal by 1.12, then round to the nearest cent.

$50.00 × 1.12 = $56.00.
