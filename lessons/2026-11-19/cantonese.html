We use Traditional Chinese characters and Jyutping with tone numbers, the Hong Kong way.

How to read Jyutping (sounds):
  • Letters show the sound. Numbers show the tone (the musical pitch): 1 high, 2 rising, 3 mid, 4 low/falling, 5 low rising, 6 low.
  • Today's words use all six tones! 舟 zau1 is tone 1 (high and flat), 海 hoi2 is tone 2 (rising), 貨 fo3 is tone 3 (middle), 湖 wu4 is tone 4 (low and falling), 艇 teng5 is tone 5 (low rising), and 換 wun6 is tone 6 (low and level). Listen for the difference!
  • Listening/speaking tip: Say each word out loud three times while looking at the characters. Tap Hear model, then Record and compare!

Voyageur tips for Ace:
  • 獨木舟 duk6 muk6 zau1 = canoe, word for word "single-wood boat," because the first canoes in many places were carved from one log. 舟 zau1 is an old word for boat.
  • 扒艇 paa4 teng5 = to paddle or row a boat. 扒 = to scoop or paddle + 艇 = small boat. In Hong Kong, 艇 is the everyday word for a little boat, and 船 (syun4) is for bigger ships.
  • 海狸 hoi2 lei4 = beaver, word for word "sea fox-cat." The beaver is one of Canada's national symbols; it's on the five-cent coin!
  • 皮毛 pei4 mou4 = fur: 皮 = skin + 毛 = hair. 交換 gaau1 wun6 = to trade or exchange: 交 = to hand over + 換 = to swap.
  • 湖 wu4 = lake, 貨物 fo3 mat6 = goods (things to sell or trade) and 冬天 dung1 tin1 = winter: 冬 = winter + 天 = day/sky. Voyageurs had to get home before 冬天!
