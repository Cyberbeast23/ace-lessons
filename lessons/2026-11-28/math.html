🍫 Math Week 62: From Bean to Bar! Chocolatier's Math Lab

Ace, every chocolate bar starts as a bitter seed inside a football-shaped pod on a cacao tree, a tree that only grows in a hot, wet belt within about 20° of the equator. More than 3 000 years ago, people in Mesoamerica (today's Mexico and Central America) were already grinding cacao into a spicy drink, and the Aztecs even used cacao beans as money. Today you're a chocolatier, a chocolate maker, following the beans from the farm to the factory to the shop. You'll use trading values, rates of change, percent decrease, percent of an amount, very large products, ratios in a recipe, the volume of a prism and a cylinder, density, the mean and a missing value, probability, unit prices, a break-even equation and percent increase.

Part 1: Learn the Moves

The 4-Step Problem Solver (a chocolatier's checklist):
  1. Read: What is the question actually asking? Underline the numbers and units (g, kg, cm, cm³, mL, °C, minutes, hours, $).
  2. Plan: Do the units match? Change hours to minutes or grams to 100-gram groups before you calculate.
  3. Solve: Do one step at a time and write each step down.
  4. Check: Is my answer reasonable? A chocolate bar doesn't weigh 10 kg, and melted chocolate isn't 300 °C!

Worked Example 1: Trading with a value list

If 1 pencil is worth 4 stickers and 1 eraser is worth 2 stickers, then 3 pencils + 5 erasers = 3 × 4 + 5 × 2 = 12 + 10 = 22 stickers. Turn everything into the same unit first, then compare.

Worked Example 2: A steady rate of change

Water at 20 °C warms by 2.5 °C every minute. After 8 minutes: 20 + 2.5 × 8 = 20 + 20 = 40 °C. Start value + (rate × time).

Worked Example 3: Percent decrease

If fruit loses 60% of its mass when dried, 40% is left. 500 g × 0.40 = 200 g. Find the percent that remains (100% − 60%), then multiply.

Worked Example 4: Multiplying big numbers in steps

A wheel turns 50 times a minute for 10 hours. 10 hours = 600 minutes, so 50 × 600 = 30 000 turns. Change the units first, then multiply.

Worked Example 5: How long will it take?

Time = change ÷ rate. Cooling from 60 °C to 30 °C is a 30-degree drop. At 3 °C per minute: 30 ÷ 3 = 10 minutes.

Worked Example 6: Ratios in a recipe

Lemonade in the ratio juice : water : sugar = 2 : 7 : 1 has 2 + 7 + 1 = 10 parts. For 800 mL, one part is 800 ÷ 10 = 80 mL, so the water is 7 × 80 = 560 mL.

Worked Example 7: Percent of an amount

A 70% dark bar means 70% of its mass comes from the cacao bean. 70% of 50 g = 0.70 × 50 = 35 g.

Worked Example 8: Volume and density

A box 10 cm × 4 cm × 2 cm has volume 10 × 4 × 2 = 80 cm³. If each cm³ has a mass of 1.5 g (its density), the mass is 80 × 1.5 = 120 g.

Worked Example 9: Volume of a cylinder, then a percent

V = π × r × r × h. A glass with radius 3 cm and height 10 cm: 3.14 × 3 × 3 × 10 = 282.6 cm³ = 282.6 mL. Filled 50% of the way: 282.6 × 0.5 = 141.3 mL.

Worked Example 10: The mean and a missing value

The mean of 6, 8 and 7 is 21 ÷ 3 = 7. To make the mean of four numbers equal 7.5, the total must be 4 × 7.5 = 30, so the fourth number is 30 − 21 = 9.

Worked Example 11: Probability as a percent

A bag holds 5 red and 15 blue marbles. P(red) = 5 ÷ 20 = 0.25 = 25%. P(not red) = 15 ÷ 20 = 75%.

Worked Example 12: Unit prices

Compare deals by finding the price for the same amount. 4 bars of 50 g for $6.00 is 200 g for $6.00, so $6.00 ÷ 200 = $0.03 per gram = $3.00 per 100 g.

Worked Example 13: Break-even equations

A stand costs $40 to run, plus $1 for each cup it makes. Cups sell for $3. Break even when money in = money out: 3n = 40 + 1n, so 2n = 40 and n = 20 cups.

Worked Example 14: Percent increase

A price goes from $2.00 to $2.50. The increase is $0.50, and 0.50 ÷ 2.00 = 0.25 = 25%. Always divide by the original amount.
