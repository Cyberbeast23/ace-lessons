Today, while your other lessons follow Tiktaalik, the fish with a neck that helps explain how fins became feet, you'll learn words for fish bodies and the move onto land: fin, gills, lungs, finger, land, shallow, ancestor and evolve. You already know 魚 (jyu2, fish), 頸 (geng2, neck), 腳 (goek3, foot / leg), 呼吸 (fu1 kap1, to breathe), 河 (ho4, river) and 化石 (faa3 sek6, fossil), so you can tell Tiktaalik's story in Cantonese! We use Traditional Chinese characters and Jyutping with tone numbers, the Hong Kong way.

How to read Jyutping (sounds):
  • Letters show the sound. Numbers show the tone (the musical pitch): 1 high, 2 rising, 3 mid, 4 low/falling, 5 low rising, 6 low.
  • Today's words use five of the six tones! 鰓 soi1 is tone 1 (high and flat), 淺 cin2 is tone 2 (rising), 肺 fai3 is tone 3 (middle), 鰭 kei4 is tone 4 (low and falling), and 陸 luk6 is tone 6 (low and level). The missing one is tone 5 (low rising), like 有 jau5, which you already know. Listen for the difference!
  • Listening/speaking tip: Say each word out loud three times while looking at the characters. Tap Hear model, then Record and compare!

Fins-to-feet tips for Ace:
  • 魚鰭 jyu4 kei4 = fin and 鰓 soi1 = gills. Look at the left side of 鰭 and 鰓: it's 魚, the fish character! Chinese characters often carry a clue about their meaning. (On its own, fish is usually said 魚 jyu2; inside words like 魚鰭 it is often jyu4.)
  • 肺 fai3 = lungs. The 月 on the left here means "body part." You can spot it in words you know: 腳 (foot), 頸 (neck), 腦 (brain) and 胃 (stomach).
  • 手指 sau2 zi2 = finger. 手 = hand and 指 = to point, so a finger is a "hand pointer"! Tiktaalik's fin had wrist bones, but no real fingers yet.
  • 陸地 luk6 dei6 = land. You know 地 from 地球 (dei6 kau4, Earth) and 地圖 (dei6 tou4, map).
  • 進化 zeon3 faa3 = to evolve. 進 = move forward and 化 = change. You know 化 from 化石 (faa3 sek6, fossil) and 消化 (siu1 faa3, to digest)!
