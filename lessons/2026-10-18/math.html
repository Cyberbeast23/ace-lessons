🌧️ Math Week 21: Weather & Storms Lab

Ace, every autumn British Columbia gets hit by atmospheric rivers: long, narrow bands of water vapour that flow across the Pacific Ocean like rivers in the sky. When one comes from near Hawaii, people call it the Pineapple Express. Meteorologists (weather scientists) turn storms into numbers: millimetres of rain, degrees Celsius, percent humidity, kilopascals of air pressure, and the percent chance of rain. Today you'll think like a forecaster with mean, median and range from a rainfall table, mm/cm/m conversions, rates, negative temperatures, percents of humidity and of normal rainfall, ratios, probability, and reading a line graph of air pressure.

Part 1: Learn the Moves

The 4-Step Problem Solver (your forecaster's checklist):
  1. Read: What is the question actually asking? Underline the numbers and units.
  2. Plan: Which operations do I need, and in what order?
  3. Solve: Do one step at a time and write each step down.
  4. Check: Is my answer reasonable? Did I answer the question that was asked, in the right units?

Worked Example 1: Mean, median and range

Rainfall on 5 days: 4, 10, 0, 18, 8 mm. Mean = total ÷ how many = 40 ÷ 5 = 8 mm.

Median: sort first (0, 4, 8, 10, 18) and take the middle value: 8 mm. Range = biggest − smallest = 18 − 0 = 18 mm.

Worked Example 2: Rain units and rates

10 mm = 1 cm and 1000 mm = 1 m. So 250 mm = 25 cm = 0.25 m.

A rate compares two different units. 36 mm of rain in 6 hours is 36 ÷ 6 = 6 mm per hour.

Handy fact: 1 mm of rain falling on 1 m² of ground = 1 litre of water. So 20 mm on a 50 m² patio = 20 × 50 = 1000 L.

Worked Example 3: Negative temperatures

From −8 °C up to 3 °C: count up 8 degrees to reach 0, then 3 more: 11 degrees. (Or: 3 − (−8) = 3 + 8 = 11.)

Mean of −5, −1 and 0: (−5 + −1 + 0) ÷ 3 = −6 ÷ 3 = −2 °C. Type a negative answer with a minus sign, like -2.

Worked Example 4: Percents in weather

Relative humidity = water vapour the air has ÷ the most it could hold × 100. If air holds 6 g but could hold 8 g: 6 ÷ 8 = 0.75 = 75%. At 100% the air is saturated and clouds or fog form.

Percent of normal: if a month normally gets 100 mm and this year got 130 mm, that is 130 ÷ 100 × 100 = 130% of normal.

Worked Example 5: Chance of rain

A forecast of "40% chance of precipitation" means that on days like this, it rains about 4 times out of 10. The chance it stays dry is 100% − 40% = 60%.

For two separate days, multiply the chances: a 50% chance on Saturday and a 50% chance on Sunday gives 0.5 × 0.5 = 0.25 = 25% for rain on both days.
