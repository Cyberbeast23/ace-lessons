✍️ Writing Week 3: Compare and Contrast (Inhale vs Exhale)

Yesterday you wrote cause-and-effect. Today, Ace, you'll level up again: write a compare-and-contrast paragraph that shows how inhaling and exhaling are alike and different — with a clear topic sentence, evidence, and linking words science writers love.

Part 1: The Lesson

Compare = alike · Contrast = different

When you compare, you show what two things share. When you contrast, you show how they differ. Strong science writing does both.

Inhale: Diaphragm moves down; chest expands; oxygen-rich air rushes in.
VS
Exhale: Diaphragm moves up; chest shrinks; carbon dioxide leaves.

Useful linking words: both, similarly, likewise, also, however, whereas, on the other hand, in contrast, while, unlike, but.

Model Compare-and-Contrast Paragraph

Topic sentenceEvidence / similarityContrast / explanationConclusion

Inhaling and exhaling are partners in every breath, yet they do opposite jobs. Both moves rely on the diaphragm and change the size of the chest cavity so air can travel through the trachea. However, when you inhale the diaphragm tightens and moves down, so oxygen-rich air rushes into the alveoli. In contrast, when you exhale the diaphragm relaxes and moves up, pushing carbon dioxide out of the lungs. Both steps happen automatically thousands of times a day, controlled by the brainstem. Therefore, a healthy breath is not one action but a smooth teamwork of in and out.

Notice: The topic sentence names both sides. Linking words like both, however, in contrast, therefore guide the reader.

For each sentence, choose whether it mainly compares (shows likeness), contrasts (shows difference), or is off-topic.
