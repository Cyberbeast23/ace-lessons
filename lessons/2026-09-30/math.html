🧮 Math Week 3: Lung Math Lab

Yesterday you counted bones. Today, Ace, you'll think like a respiratory doctor: breathing rates, lung capacity in litres, oxygen percentages, inhaler doses, PE runs, and hospital wait times — using fractions, ratios, percentages, BEDMAS, mean/median/range, unit rates, area/volume, and elapsed time.

Part 1: Learn the Moves

The 4-Step Problem Solver (your doctor's check-up):
  1. Read: What is the question actually asking? Underline the numbers and units.
  2. Plan: Which operations do I need, and in what order?
  3. Solve: Do one step at a time and write each step down.
  4. Check: Is my answer reasonable? Did I answer the question that was asked?

Worked Example 1: Breathing rate over time

At rest, Ace breathes 15 times per minute. How many breaths does he take in 8 minutes?

Step 1: Rate × time = 15 × 8 = 120 breaths.

Check: 120 ÷ 8 = 15 ✔

Worked Example 2: Fractions of lung capacity

An adult lung can hold about 6 L of air. Ace's lung capacity is 2/3 of that. How many litres can Ace's lungs hold?

Step 1: 2/3 × 6 = 4 L.

Check: 4 is less than 6, and two-thirds feels right ✔

Worked Example 3: Oxygen percentage

Air is about 21% oxygen. How many millilitres of oxygen are in 1500 mL of air?

Step 1: 21% = 0.21.

Step 2: 0.21 × 1500 = 315 mL.

Worked Example 4: Mean, median, and range

Five patients waited for a breathing test: 12, 30, 18, 15, and 20 minutes. Find the mean, median, and range.

Mean = (12 + 30 + 18 + 15 + 20) ÷ 5 = 95 ÷ 5 = 19 minutes.

Ordered: 12, 15, 18, 20, 30. Median (middle) = 18 minutes.

Range = 30 − 12 = 18 minutes.

Worked Example 5: Unit conversion, volume, and BEDMAS

Convert 3500 mL to litres: 3500 ÷ 1000 = 3.5 L.

A storage box for inhalers is 12 cm × 5 cm × 4 cm. Volume = 12 × 5 × 4 = 240 cm³.

BEDMAS: (40 − 8) ÷ 4 × 3² + 2 → 32 ÷ 4 = 8; 3² = 9; 8 × 9 = 72; 72 + 2 = 74.
