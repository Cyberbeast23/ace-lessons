Worked Example 1: Nerve-signal speed (distance, speed, time)

A nerve impulse is still a speed problem: distance = speed × time, time = distance ÷ speed, and speed = distance ÷ time.

A myelinated (insulated) motor neuron can carry a signal at about 100 m/s. From your spinal cord to your toe is roughly 1.5 m: time = 1.5 ÷ 100 = 0.015 s, which is 15 milliseconds (ms) because 1 s = 1000 ms.

Shortcut: to change seconds into milliseconds, multiply by 1000. To change ms into seconds, divide by 1000.

Worked Example 2: Mean, median and range

Scientists measure reaction time (how long from a flash of light until you press a button) many times, then summarise the data.

Mean (average) = sum ÷ count. For 200, 180, 220 ms: (200 + 180 + 220) ÷ 3 = 200 ms.

Median = the middle value after sorting. Sorted: 180, 200, 220 → median 200 ms. With an even count, average the two middle values.

Range = highest − lowest. 220 − 180 = 40 ms.

Worked Example 3: Ratios and percents

A ratio compares two quantities. 40 sensory neurons to 10 motor neurons simplifies to 4 : 1 (divide both by 10).

A percent is a special ratio out of 100. If 3 out of 5 messages are sensory: 3 ÷ 5 × 100 = 60%.

Percent change = (new − old) ÷ old × 100. Helmets cut serious head injuries from 50 cases to 20: decrease = 30, and 30 ÷ 50 × 100 = 60% decrease.

Worked Example 4: Powers of ten and huge neuron counts

A human brain has about 86 billion neurons. In scientific notation, 86 000 000 000 = 8.6 × 1010 (move the decimal 10 places left from 86 000 000 000 to get 8.6).

Multiplying powers of ten: 10a × 10b = 10a+b. Dividing: 10a ÷ 10b = 10a−b. So 1011 ÷ 103 = 108.

Worked Example 5: Sleep as a fraction of a day

There are 24 hours in a day. If Ace sleeps 8 hours: 8 ÷ 24 = 1/3, which is about 33% (exactly 33⅓%). Memory consolidates during sleep, so those hours matter for learning!
