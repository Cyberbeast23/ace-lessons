How to read Jyutping (sounds):
  • Letters show the sound. Numbers show the tone (the musical pitch): 1 high, 2 rising, 3 mid, 4 low/falling, 5 low rising, 6 low.
  • Example: zi2 in 紙 (paper) — say zi with tone 2 (rising), sliding up like you are asking a question: "zi?"
  • Listening/speaking tip: Say each word out loud three times while looking at the characters. Tap Hear model, then Record and compare!

Ancient China tips for Ace:
  • 中國 zung1 gwok3 means China. 中 (zung1) means middle and 國 (gwok3) means country, so 中國 is the "Middle Country." You met 國 before in 國家 (gwok3 gaa1), country.
  • 長城 coeng4 sing4 means the Great Wall. 長 (coeng4) means long and 城 (sing4) means a city wall, so it is the "Long Wall." 長城好長！ = The Great Wall is very long!
  • 紙 zi2 means paper and 筆 bat1 means pen. Chinese uses measure words: 一張紙 (jat1 zoeng1 zi2) = a sheet of paper (張 is for flat things), and 一支筆 (jat1 zi1 bat1) = a pen (支 is for stick-shaped things). An old Chinese writing brush is 毛筆 (mou4 bat1), a "hair pen"!
  • 寫字 se2 zi6 means to write (characters). 寫 (se2) = write and 字 (zi6) = a character or word. Chinese characters are 中文字 (zung1 man4 zi6).
  • 書 syu1 means book. 睇書 (tai2 syu1) = to read a book, and a library is 圖書館 (tou4 syu1 gun2), a "picture-and-book building."
  • 皇帝 wong4 dai3 means emperor. The First Emperor is called 秦始皇 (ceon4 ci2 wong4) in Chinese.
  • 以前 ji5 cin4 means before or in the past. Its opposite is 而家 (ji4 gaa1), now. 以前冇紙 = In the past there was no paper! 📜
