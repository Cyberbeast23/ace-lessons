You already know 水 (seoi2, water), 落雨 (lok6 jyu5, to rain), 山 (saan1, mountain), 河 (ho4, river) and 雪 (syut3, snow), so you can follow a raindrop all the way from the mountain to your glass! We use Traditional Chinese characters and Jyutping with tone numbers, the Hong Kong way.

How to read Jyutping (sounds):
  • Letters show the sound. Numbers show the tone (the musical pitch): 1 high, 2 rising, 3 mid, 4 low/falling, 5 low rising, 6 low.
  • Example: 煲 bou1 is tone 1, high and flat, but 塘 tong4 is tone 4, low and falling. 淨 zeng6 is tone 6, low and level, and 水 seoi2 is tone 2, rising from the middle. Listen for the difference!
  • Listening/speaking tip: Say each word out loud three times while looking at the characters. Tap Hear model, then Record and compare!

Clean water tips for Ace:
  • Six of today's eight words contain 水 seoi2 (water), a word you learned in your food lesson. Spot it in 水喉, 飲水, 水管, 水塘, 煲水 and 慳水!
  • 水喉 seoi2 hau4 = tap. 喉 means "throat," so a tap is the water's throat! 開水喉 hoi1 seoi2 hau4 = turn on the tap; 閂水喉 saan1 seoi2 hau4 = turn off the tap. Tap water is 水喉水 seoi2 hau4 seoi2.
  • 乾淨 gon1 zeng6 (clean) and 污糟 wu1 zou1 (dirty) are opposites. Say them as a pair: 乾淨 ✔ 污糟 ✘.
  • 煲水 bou1 seoi2 = boil water (煲 is also the word for a cooking pot). During a boil-water advisory, people must 煲水 before drinking. Water that has been boiled is 滾水 gwan2 seoi2.
  • 慳 haan1 means "to save, not waste." You learned 電 (din6, electricity), so can you guess 慳電? It's haan1 din6, save electricity!
