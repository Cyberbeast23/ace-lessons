We use Traditional Chinese characters and Jyutping with tone numbers, the Hong Kong way.

How to read Jyutping (sounds):
  • Letters show the sound. Numbers show the tone (the musical pitch): 1 high, 2 rising, 3 mid, 4 low/falling, 5 low rising, 6 low.
  • Example: 驚 geng1 (scared) is tone 1, high and flat, while 靜 zing6 (quiet) is tone 6, low and level. 南瓜 naam4 gwaa1 (pumpkin) starts low and falling (tone 4), then jumps up high and flat (tone 1).
  • Listening/speaking tip: Say each word out loud three times while looking at the characters. Tap Hear model, then Record and compare!

Night tips for Ace:
  • 夜晚 je6 maan5 = night. Its opposite is 日頭 (jat6 tau2, daytime). 今晚 (gam1 maan5) = tonight.
  • 蝙蝠 pin1 fuk1 = bat. In Chinese culture bats are actually lucky, because 蝠 (fuk1) sounds just like 福 (fuk1, good luck). That's why you see bats on old Chinese decorations!
  • 貓頭鷹 maau1 tau4 jing1 = owl, literally "cat-head eagle." 貓 = cat, 頭 = head (you learned 頭 on body day!), 鷹 = eagle. An owl's round face does look like a cat's!
  • 萬聖節 maan6 sing3 zit3 = Halloween: 萬 = ten thousand, 聖 = saint, 節 = festival. 南瓜 naam4 gwaa1 = pumpkin, literally "southern melon."
  • 驚 geng1 = scared. 我好驚！ = I'm so scared! 我唔驚！ = I'm not scared! (唔 m4 = not). 聽到 teng1 dou2 = hear; 聽唔到 (teng1 m4 dou2) = can't hear.
