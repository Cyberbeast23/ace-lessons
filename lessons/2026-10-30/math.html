🦇 Math Week 33: Night Hunter's Math Lab

Ace, when the sun goes down, a whole hidden world wakes up. Bats find moths in total darkness by listening to echoes, owls swoop on mice they can only hear, and both animals have bodies built for the night. Scientists who study them use a lot of math: the speed of sound, sound frequencies, heart rates, compass bearings and population counts. Today you'll work like a wildlife biologist with echo distances, kilohertz, percent increase, compass bearings, the median, elapsed time across midnight, km/h to m/s, the distance between two points on a coordinate grid, and a three-part equation.

Part 1: Learn the Moves

The 4-Step Problem Solver (your field biologist's checklist):
  1. Read: What is the question actually asking? Underline the numbers and units.
  2. Plan: Which operations do I need, and in what order? Do the units match (Hz with Hz, minutes with minutes)?
  3. Solve: Do one step at a time and write each step down.
  4. Check: Is my answer reasonable? Did I answer the question that was asked, in the right units?

Worked Example 1: Echo distance (there and back!)

An echo travels to an object and back. Distance travelled by the sound = speed × time. The object is only half that far away.

Sound moves about 340 m/s. An echo returns after 0.1 s: 340 × 0.1 = 34 m of travel, so the wall is 34 ÷ 2 = 17 m away.

Worked Example 2: Hertz and kilohertz

Hertz (Hz) means vibrations per second. kilo means 1000, so 1 kHz = 1000 Hz.

A whistle at 6 kHz = 6000 Hz. Compared with a 2000 Hz note, it is 6000 ÷ 2000 = 3 times higher.

Worked Example 3: Percent increase

Percent increase = (new − old) ÷ old × 100. Always divide by the old (starting) value.

From 40 to 70: (70 − 40) ÷ 40 = 0.75, so a 75% increase. Going from 10 to 20 is a 100% increase (it doubled), not 200%!

Worked Example 4: Compass bearings

A bearing is measured clockwise from north: north = 0°, east = 090°, south = 180°, west = 270°.

Turning 60° counter-clockwise from north takes you to 360° − 60° = 300°.

Worked Example 5: The median

The median is the middle value after you put the data in order. With an odd number of values there is one exact middle.

8, 2, 5, 9, 3 → in order: 2, 3, 5, 8, 9. Median = 5.

Worked Example 6: Elapsed time across midnight

Split the time at midnight. From 9:30 p.m. to midnight is 2 h 30 min = 150 min. From midnight to 1:15 a.m. is 75 min. Total = 225 min.

Worked Example 7: km/h back to m/s

Last lesson you multiplied m/s by 3.6 to get km/h. To go the other way, divide km/h by 3.6.

72 km/h ÷ 3.6 = 20 m/s.

Worked Example 8: Distance between two points on a grid

Find how far apart the points are across (difference in x) and up (difference in y). Those are the two legs of a right triangle, so use a² + b² = c².

From (1, 2) to (4, 6): across 4 − 1 = 3, up 6 − 2 = 4. 3² + 4² = 25, √25 = 5 units. Careful with negatives: from −3 to 2 is 5 steps, because 2 − (−3) = 5.

Worked Example 9: Three amounts, one equation

"A has x, B has twice as many as A, and C has 1 more than A. Together they have 21." So x + 2x + (x + 1) = 21 → 4x + 1 = 21 → 4x = 20 → x = 5. Check: 5 + 10 + 6 = 21 ✔
