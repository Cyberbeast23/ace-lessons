♻️ Math Week 59: Trash Talk! Waste Detective's Math Lab

Ace, every time you toss something "away," it has to go somewhere. In Canada, each person throws out hundreds of kilograms of waste every year, and every city and town has to decide what to do with it: bury it in a landfill, burn it, recycle it, or compost it. Today you're a waste detective working for your community. You'll use dividing large numbers, fractions of an amount, percent and percent decrease, elapsed years, volume of a rectangular prism (m³ and litres), rates and ratios, money, the mean, probability of two events without putting the first one back, multiplying large numbers, and two-step equations.

Part 1: Learn the Moves

The 4-Step Problem Solver (a waste detective's checklist):
  1. Read: What is the question actually asking? Underline the numbers and units (kg, tonnes, m³, litres, years, ¢ and $).
  2. Plan: Do the units match? Change tonnes to kilograms (× 1 000) or cubic metres to litres (× 1 000) before you compare or divide.
  3. Solve: Do one step at a time and write each step down.
  4. Check: Is my answer reasonable? A family doesn't make 5 000 kg of garbage a week, and a kitchen bin doesn't hold 9 000 litres!

Worked Example 1: Dividing big numbers by cancelling zeros

6 000 000 kg shared among 15 000 people: cross off three zeros from each: 6 000 ÷ 15 = 400 kg per person. (1 tonne = 1 000 kg.)

Worked Example 2: A fraction of an amount

3/8 of 24 kg: find 1/8 first (24 ÷ 8 = 3 kg), then multiply: 3 × 3 = 9 kg.

Worked Example 3: Percent of a total

If 18 of 45 kg could be recycled, that's 18 ÷ 45 = 0.4 = 40%.

Worked Example 4: Percent decrease

From 25 kg down to 20 kg: the decrease is 5 kg. Divide by the starting amount: 5 ÷ 25 = 0.2 = 20% decrease.

Worked Example 5: Elapsed years

From 2010 to 2026 is 2026 − 2010 = 16 years. If something needs 100 years in total, there are 100 − 16 = 84 years left.

Worked Example 6: Volume of a rectangular prism

Volume = length × width × height. A box 0.5 m × 0.4 m × 0.5 m holds 0.1 m³. Since 1 m³ = 1 000 L, that's 100 L.

Worked Example 7: Rates: how many days?

A pit holds 9 000 m³. Trucks add 300 m³ a day. It fills in 9 000 ÷ 300 = 30 days.

Worked Example 8: Money with cents

Each can returns 10¢ = $0.10. So 75 cans × $0.10 = $7.50.

Worked Example 9: Ratios

A 2 : 1 ratio means 2 parts of one thing for every 1 part of the other. With 5 L of the "1 part," you need 5 × 2 = 10 L of the "2 parts."

Worked Example 10: The mean (average)

Add all the values, then divide by how many there are. The mean of 4.5, 6.0 and 7.5 is 18.0 ÷ 3 = 6.

Worked Example 11: Probability without replacement

A bag has 3 blue and 2 red cards. Pick one, keep it out, pick again. P(both red) = 2/5 × 1/4 = 2/20 = 1/10. The second fraction changes because there's one fewer red card and one fewer card in total!

Worked Example 12: Multiplying large numbers

300 000 × 4 × 50: 3 × 4 × 5 = 60, then count the zeros (5 + 1 = 6): 60 000 000 = 60 million.

Worked Example 13: Two-step equations

"9 more than 0.5 times a number is 15" means 0.5n + 9 = 15. Undo in reverse order: 15 − 9 = 6, then 6 ÷ 0.5 = 12.
