Today, while your other lessons explore kitchen chemistry, you'll learn kitchen and flavour words: kitchen, to cook, sour, salty, salt, vinegar, sugar, and tasty. A chemist would notice something cool: 酸 (syun1, sour) is also the Cantonese word scientists use for an acid, because acids taste sour! You already know 甜 (tim4, sweet), 水 (seoi2, water), and 飯 (faan6, rice), so listen for them in today's sentences. We use Traditional Chinese characters and Jyutping with tone numbers, the Hong Kong way.

How to read Jyutping (sounds):
  • Letters show the sound. Numbers show the tone (the musical pitch): 1 high, 2 rising, 3 mid, 4 low/falling, 5 low rising, 6 low.
  • Example: syun1 酸 (sour) is tone 1 (high and flat), while haam4 鹹 (salty) is tone 4 (low and falling). Try saying them one after the other: high "syuuun" for a lemon, then low, falling "haaam" for the sea! 糖 tong4 and 鹽 jim4 are tone 4 too, so sugar and salt both fall low.
  • Listening/speaking tip: Say each word out loud three times while looking at the characters. Tap Hear model, then Record and compare!

Kitchen tips for Ace:
  • 廚房 cyu4 fong2 means kitchen. Dictionaries write 房 as fong4 (room), but in everyday Hong Kong speech 廚房 ends with a rising fong2. Both are accepted in today's typing quiz.
  • 煮 zyu2 means to cook or to boil. 煮飯 (zyu2 faan6) = to cook rice, and also just "to cook a meal."
  • 酸 syun1 = sour. In science class, an acid is 酸 too! Baking soda is 梳打粉 (so1 daa2 fan2), "soda powder."
  • 鹹 haam4 = salty and 鹽 jim4 = salt. 海水好鹹 = seawater is very salty.
  • 醋 cou3 = vinegar (tone 3, mid and level) and 糖 tong4 = sugar.
  • 好味 hou2 mei6 = tasty. You already know 好食 (hou2 sik6), "good to eat." Both are great to say to the cook! A kitchen chemist's sentence: 醋加梳打粉，有好多泡泡！ (cou3 gaa1 so1 daa2 fan2, jau5 hou2 do1 paau1 paau1!) = Vinegar plus baking soda makes lots of bubbles! 🫧
