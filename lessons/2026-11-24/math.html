🦴 Math Week 58: Wings of the Mesozoic! Pterosaur Flight Lab

Ace, long before the first bird flapped its wings, the skies belonged to pterosaurs (say it TERR-oh-sore; the p is silent!). They were reptiles, close cousins of the dinosaurs but not dinosaurs, and they were the first animals with backbones to truly fly. Some were as small as a sparrow; the biggest had a wingspan wider than a small airplane. Today you're an engineer in a pterosaur flight lab. You'll use dividing large numbers, timelines in millions of years, scale and ratios, fractions of a length, unit conversions (m, cm, mm, m/s, km/h), area of a triangle, rates such as kilograms per square metre, percent, scale models, the mean, probability of two events and two-step equations.

Part 1: Learn the Moves

The 4-Step Problem Solver (a flight engineer's checklist):
  1. Read: What is the question actually asking? Underline the numbers and units (mm, cm, m, km, m², kg, m/s, km/h, millions of years).
  2. Plan: Do the units match? Change metres to centimetres, or millimetres to centimetres, before you compare or divide.
  3. Solve: Do one step at a time and write each step down.
  4. Check: Is my answer reasonable? A pterosaur's wing bone isn't 40 m long, and nothing in the Mesozoic flew at 5 000 km/h!

Worked Example 1: Dividing big numbers by cancelling zeros

How many times bigger is 8 000 000 than 400 000? Cross off the same number of zeros from each: 8 000 000 ÷ 400 000 = 80 ÷ 4 = 20.

Worked Example 2: Timelines in millions of years

"Millions of years ago" counts back from today, so the bigger number is older. From 200 million years ago to 150 million years ago is 200 − 150 = 50 million years. On a timeline where 1 cm = 5 million years, that's 50 ÷ 5 = 10 cm.

Worked Example 3: How many times longer? (ratios)

To compare two lengths, divide: a 6 m rope is 6 ÷ 1.5 = 4 times as long as a 1.5 m rope. Both lengths must be in the same unit first!

Worked Example 4: A fraction of a length

2/5 of 4.5 m: find 1/5 first (4.5 ÷ 5 = 0.9 m), then double it: 0.9 × 2 = 1.8 m = 180 cm.

Worked Example 5: Millimetres, centimetres and metres

1 cm = 10 mm and 1 m = 100 cm. To compare 5 cm with 2.5 mm, change 5 cm to 50 mm: 50 ÷ 2.5 = 20 times.

Worked Example 6: Area of a triangle

Area = base × height ÷ 2. A triangle with a base of 2 m and a height of 0.6 m has an area of 2 × 0.6 ÷ 2 = 0.6 m².

Worked Example 7: A rate: kilograms per square metre

Engineers divide mass by wing area to get wing loading. A glider of 90 kg with 15 m² of wing has 90 ÷ 15 = 6 kg/m². Lower wing loading usually means slower, easier gliding.

Worked Example 8: Metres per second to kilometres per hour

1 hour = 3 600 s, and 1 km = 1 000 m. So 1 m/s = 3 600 m per hour = 3.6 km/h. To convert, multiply by 3.6: 10 m/s × 3.6 = 36 km/h.

Worked Example 9: Distance = speed × time

At 5 m/s for 2 hours: 2 h = 7 200 s, and 5 × 7 200 = 36 000 m = 36 km.

Worked Example 10: Percent of a total

If 9 of 60 fossils are teeth, that's 9 ÷ 60 = 0.15 = 15%.

Worked Example 11: Scale models

On a 1 : 10 model, every real length is divided by 10. A real 4 m wing becomes 4 ÷ 10 = 0.4 m = 40 cm.

Worked Example 12: The mean (average)

Add all the values, then divide by how many there are. The mean of 2, 3 and 7 is (2 + 3 + 7) ÷ 3 = 12 ÷ 3 = 4.

Worked Example 13: Probability of two events

If you pick a marble, put it back, then pick again, the two picks don't affect each other, so multiply. With 2 red out of 10: 2/10 × 2/10 = 4/100 (which simplifies to 1/25).

Worked Example 14: Two-step equations

"3 more than 6 times a number is 21" means 6n + 3 = 21. Undo in reverse order: 21 − 3 = 18, then 18 ÷ 6 = 3.
