🦷 Math Week 55: Open Wide! Dental Detective's Math Lab

Ace, a dental office runs on numbers. Dentists and dental hygienists count teeth, measure gums in millimetres, read X-rays that are enlarged on a screen, schedule appointments to the minute, and figure out how much fluoride is in a town's tap water. Today you're joining the team as a dental detective. You'll use percent of a total, minutes-to-hours conversions, dividing decimals, rounding down when you need whole items, rates in mg per litre, scale factors, the mean with mixed units, percent of an amount, probability with "not", elapsed time and scheduling, the volume of a cylinder, working backward from a percent discount, two-step equations and multi-step rate problems.

Part 1: Learn the Moves

The 4-Step Problem Solver (your dental detective's checklist):
  1. Read: What is the question actually asking? Underline the numbers and units (mm, cm, m, mL, L, g, mg, min, h, $, %).
  2. Plan: Do the units match? Change metres to centimetres or minutes to hours before you calculate.
  3. Solve: Do one step at a time and write each step down.
  4. Check: Is my answer reasonable? Nobody has 50 teeth, and a family can't pay more than the whole bill.

Worked Example 1: Percent of a total

If 18 out of 24 students brushed this morning, that's 18 ÷ 24 = 0.75 = 75%.

Worked Example 2: Minutes to hours

There are 60 minutes in an hour. 3 minutes a day for 100 days is 300 minutes, and 300 ÷ 60 = 5 hours.

Worked Example 3: Dividing with decimals

A granola bar has 14 g of sugar. If 1 teaspoon of sugar is about 4 g, that's 14 ÷ 4 = 3.5 teaspoons.

Worked Example 4: Rounding down for whole items

A 10 m roll of ribbon is cut into 30 cm pieces. 10 m = 1 000 cm, and 1 000 ÷ 30 = 33.3…, so you get 33 whole pieces. You can't use the leftover bit as a full piece, so round down.

Worked Example 5: Rates in mg per litre

A rate like 2 mg/L means 2 milligrams in every litre. In 3 L there are 3 × 2 = 6 mg. In half a litre there are 0.5 × 2 = 1 mg.

Worked Example 6: Scale factor

A drawing of a tooth is 12 cm tall, but the real tooth is 2 cm tall. The drawing is 12 ÷ 2 = 6 times as big as the real thing. (Scale factor = image size ÷ real size.)

Worked Example 7: The mean with mixed units

To average 1 min, 40 s and 1 min 20 s, change everything to seconds first: 60 + 40 + 80 = 180 s, and 180 ÷ 3 = 60 s.

Worked Example 8: Percent of an amount

If a bill is $200 and insurance pays 70%, the insurance pays 0.7 × 200 = $140, and the family pays the other 30%: 200 − 140 = $60.

Worked Example 9: Probability with "not"

If 6 of 20 marbles are red, then 20 − 6 = 14 are not red. P(not red) = 14/20 = 7/10. (Or: 1 − 6/20.)

Worked Example 10: Elapsed time and scheduling

From 9:00 a.m. to 1:30 p.m. is 4 h 30 min = 270 min. If each visit is 45 min, that's 270 ÷ 45 = 6 visits.

Worked Example 11: Volume of a cylinder

Volume = π × r × r × h. A cup with radius 2 cm and height 5 cm: 3.14 × 2 × 2 × 5 = 62.8 cm³. Since 1 cm³ = 1 mL, it holds 62.8 mL.

Worked Example 12: Working backward from a discount

A sale price is 20% off, so you pay 80% (0.8) of the original. If the sale price is $40: original × 0.8 = 40, so original = 40 ÷ 0.8 = $50.

Worked Example 13: Two-step equations

"3 more than twice a number is 13" means 2n + 3 = 13. Undo the steps in reverse order: 13 − 3 = 10, then 10 ÷ 2 = 5.

Worked Example 14: Multi-step rate problems

If one person needs a new item every 4 months, then in 1 year (12 months) they need 12 ÷ 4 = 3. A family of 5 needs 5 × 3 = 15 a year.
