Today, while your other lessons explore how skyscrapers stand tall, you'll learn tall-building words: skyscraper, elevator, stairs, floor (storey), top floor, foundation, architect and engineer. Hong Kong has more skyscrapers than almost any city on Earth, so these words are used there every day! You already know 大 (daai6, big) and 好高 (hou2 gou1, very tall), so now you can describe a whole skyline. We use Traditional Chinese characters and Jyutping with tone numbers, the Hong Kong way.

How to read Jyutping (sounds):
  • Letters show the sound. Numbers show the tone (the musical pitch): 1 high, 2 rising, 3 mid, 4 low/falling, 5 low rising, 6 low.
  • Today's words use five of the six tones: 天 tin1 is tone 1 (high and flat), 頂 deng2 is tone 2 (rising), 降 gong3 is tone 3 (middle), 樓 lau4 is tone 4 (low and falling), and 地 dei6 is tone 6 (low and flat). Listen closely to 樓 lau4: it drops low, like an elevator going down to the lobby!
  • Listening/speaking tip: Say each word out loud three times while looking at the characters. Tap Hear model, then Record and compare!

Building tips for Ace:
  • 摩天大樓 mo1 tin1 daai6 lau4 = skyscraper: 摩 = to rub + 天 = sky + 大樓 = big building. A skyscraper is a "big building that rubs the sky." English says scraper, Cantonese says rubber: same idea!
  • 樓 lau4 means a building or a floor. It's in 樓梯 lau4 tai1 (stairs: 梯 = ladder, so stairs are a "building ladder") and 頂樓 deng2 lau4 (top floor: 頂 = top).
  • 升降機 sing1 gong3 gei1 = elevator: 升 = go up + 降 = go down + 機 = machine. It's literally an "up-down machine"!
  • 層 cang4 counts floors or layers: 八十層 (baat3 sap6 cang4) = eighty storeys.
  • 師 si1 means an expert or master. 建築 (gin3 zuk1, building / architecture) + 師 = 建築師, architect. 工程 (gung1 cing4, engineering project) + 師 = 工程師, engineer. You already know a word like this: 醫生 is a doctor, and 牙醫 is a dentist. 地基 dei6 gei1 = foundation: 地 = ground + 基 = base.
