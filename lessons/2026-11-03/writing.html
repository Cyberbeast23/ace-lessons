✍️ Writing Week 37: Travel Brochure: Visit Castle Ironhold! 🏰🗺️

Yesterday you wrote like a poet. Today, Ace, you'll write like a tourism writer. A travel brochure is a short, eye-catching guide that does two jobs at once: it informs (true facts, history, what to see, how to get there) and it persuades (it makes readers want to visit right now). Today you'll invent your own medieval castle that is now open to visitors, and write a brochure that is packed with real castle history and clever persuasive language.

Part 1: The Lesson

The six parts of a great brochure 🧰

Brochure readers skim, so every part must grab attention fast. Headings and short sections help a reader find what they want.

1. Title + slogan: a bold name and a short, catchy line ("Castle Ironhold: 700 Years of Standing Strong!")
2. Welcome hook: an opening that speaks to the reader as "you" and sparks curiosity ("Have you ever stood where archers once stood?")
3. History section: true, specific facts that explain why the place matters (dates, people, events)
4. Top attractions: a list of the best things to see and do, each with a vivid description
5. Visitor information: practical facts: hours, prices, how to get there, tips
6. Call to action: a final line telling readers exactly what to do next ("Book your tour today!")

Five persuasive moves

  • Talk to the reader: use you and your, so readers picture themselves there.
  • Use sensory words: help them see, hear, smell and feel the place ("the clank of the iron portcullis").
  • Use power words: exciting adjectives and verbs (towering, legendary, explore, discover), but don't overdo it!
  • Use a rhetorical question: a question that doesn't need an answer, just a "yes!" in the reader's head.
  • Prove it with facts and numbers: "walls 3 metres thick" persuades better than "really thick walls."

Honest-brochure rule: persuade, but never trick. Your castle is imaginary, but every history fact you include (how portcullises, moats and trebuchets worked) must be true.

Model Brochure (short version): "Castle Ironhold"

Title, slogan + welcome hookHistory sectionTop attractionsVisitor info + call to action

CASTLE IRONHOLD: Seven Centuries of Standing Strong!
Have you ever wanted to walk where knights once marched? Step through our towering gatehouse and travel back to the Middle Ages.
Our Story: Masons finished Castle Ironhold in 1283. Its curtain wall is 3 metres thick, and in 1316 it survived a 50-day siege because its defenders never ran out of water from the courtyard well.
Don't Miss: Hear the clank of our working portcullis as it thunders down. Climb 60 spiral steps to the top of the round tower. Watch our full-size trebuchet fling a pumpkin across the moat!
Plan Your Visit: Open daily, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. Kids 12 and under: $8. Wear comfy shoes; the cobblestones are bumpy! Lower the drawbridge on your next adventure, and book your tickets today!

Notice: the writer speaks to "you"; uses headings readers can skim; mixes real castle facts (portcullis, siege, spiral stairs) with exact numbers; uses sound and power words (clank, thunders, towering); and ends by telling the reader what to do.
