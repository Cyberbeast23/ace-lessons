We use Traditional Chinese characters and Jyutping with tone numbers, the Hong Kong way.

How to read Jyutping (sounds):
  • Letters show the sound. Numbers show the tone (the musical pitch): 1 high, 2 rising, 3 mid, 4 low/falling, 5 low rising, 6 low.
  • Example: 馬 maa5 (horse) is tone 5, low and rising, but 媽 maa1 (mom) is tone 1, high and flat. Same sound, different tone, totally different meaning, so don't call your mom a horse!
  • Listening/speaking tip: Say each word out loud three times while looking at the characters. Tap Hear model, then Record and compare!

Castle tips for Ace:
  • 城堡 sing4 bou2 = castle. Both characters have 土 (earth) on the left or bottom, because castles were built from earth and stone. 城 on its own means a walled city.
  • 國王 gwok3 wong4 = king, literally "country king." 王 (wong4) means king. 皇后 wong4 hau6 = queen. Notice 王 hiding at the bottom of 皇!
  • 騎士 ke4 si6 = knight, literally "riding person." 騎 (ke4) = to ride, and it has 馬 (horse) on the left! 騎馬 (ke4 maa5) = ride a horse.
  • 劍 gim3 = sword. The two strokes on the right (刂) are the "knife" sign, which shows up in many cutting words.
  • 牆 coeng4 = wall and 塔 taap3 = tower. 塔 also has 土 (earth) on the left, like 城堡. 高塔 (gou1 taap3) = a tall tower.
