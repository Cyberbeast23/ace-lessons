🌟 Math Week 49: Star Stories: Astronomer's Math Lab

Ace, every star you see at night is a gigantic ball of glowing gas, and many of them are bigger and brighter than our Sun. Stars are so far away that astronomers measure their distances in light-years: the distance light travels in one year, about 9.5 trillion kilometres! Light is the fastest thing in the universe, racing along at about 300 000 km every second. Today you are an astronomer at an observatory. You'll use time = distance ÷ speed, powers of ten, integer differences, multi-step percents, scale factors, how volume and area grow when you scale something up, the mean and the median, a two-step equation, the area of a circle, probability, and a multi-step "time machine" problem.

Part 1: Learn the Moves

The 4-Step Problem Solver (your astronomer's checklist):
  1. Read: What is the question actually asking? Underline the numbers and units (mm, cm, m, km, m², km/s, °C, s, years, light-years, %).
  2. Plan: Do the units match? Change metres to centimetres or hours to minutes before you answer.
  3. Solve: Do one step at a time and write each step down.
  4. Check: Is my answer reasonable? Light from the Moon reaches us in about a second, not in a week!

Worked Example 1: Time = distance ÷ speed

If you know how far and how fast, divide to find how long. Light travels about 300 000 km/s.

Light crossing 900 000 km takes 900 000 ÷ 300 000 = 3 seconds. Tip: cross off the same number of zeros first: 9 000 ÷ 3 000 = 9 ÷ 3.

Worked Example 2: Powers of ten and "scientific notation"

Astronomers write giant numbers as a number × a power of ten. 1012 is a 1 with 12 zeros (one trillion).

One light-year ≈ 9.5 × 1012 km = 9.5 trillion km. So 2 light-years ≈ 2 × 9.5 = 19 trillion km. Multiply the front numbers and keep the power of ten!

Worked Example 3: Difference between a positive and a negative

Difference = higher − lower. From −100 °C up to 400 °C: 400 − (−100) = 400 + 100 = 500 degrees. Subtracting a negative means adding.

Worked Example 4: The "leftover" percent

If 60% of a bag is red and 30% is blue, the rest is 100 − 60 − 30 = 10%. 10% of 500 g = 0.10 × 500 = 50 g.

Worked Example 5: Scaling up (length, area, volume)

If you make a ball 2 times wider, its area (like a mirror or a shadow) grows 2 × 2 = 4 times, and its volume grows 2 × 2 × 2 = 8 times.

Rule: scale factor k → area × k², volume × k³. A ball 3 times wider holds 3³ = 27 times as much!

Worked Example 6: Mean vs. median

Mean = total ÷ how many. Median = the middle value after you put the numbers in order. With an even count, the median is the mean of the two middle numbers.

2, 9, 4, 7 → order: 2, 4, 7, 9. Mean = 22 ÷ 4 = 5.5. Median = (4 + 7) ÷ 2 = 5.5.

Worked Example 7: Two-step equation

Solve 12w + 30 = 90. Undo the adding first: 12w = 60. Then undo the multiplying: w = 60 ÷ 12 = 5. Check: 12 × 5 + 30 = 90 ✔

Worked Example 8: Area of a circle

A = π × r × r (use π ≈ 3.14; r = half the diameter). A mirror 2 m across: r = 1 m, A = 3.14 × 1 × 1 = 3.14 m².

Worked Example 9: Probability

Probability = favourable outcomes ÷ total outcomes, in lowest terms. 6 blue stars out of 24 → 6/24 = 1/4. "Not blue" = 18/24 = 3/4.
