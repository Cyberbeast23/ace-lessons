🧮 Math Week 5: Ancient Egypt Math Lab

Sail up the Nile with me, Ace! Today you'll use fractions, ratios, percentages, BEDMAS, mean/median/range, unit rates, area/volume, scale, and elapsed time — just like a surveyor measuring pyramids, temples, and grain stores in Ancient Egypt.

Part 1: Learn the Moves

The 4-Step Problem Solver (your temple check-up):
  1. Read: What is the question actually asking? Underline the numbers and units.
  2. Plan: Which operations do I need, and in what order?
  3. Solve: Do one step at a time and write each step down.
  4. Check: Is my answer reasonable? Did I answer the question that was asked?

Worked Example 1: Pyramid scale model

A museum model of a pyramid is built at a scale of 1 : 50. The model is 2.8 m tall. How tall was the real pyramid in metres?

Step 1: Real height = model × scale factor = 2.8 × 50 = 140 m.

Check: 140 ÷ 50 = 2.8 ✔

Worked Example 2: Fractions of grain

A storehouse holds 96 sacks of grain. Workers have moved 5/8 of them. How many sacks have been moved?

Step 1: 96 ÷ 8 = 12; 12 × 5 = 60 sacks.

Worked Example 3: Percent of temple stones

Out of 80 carved stones, 35% show hieroglyphs. How many stones show hieroglyphs?

Step 1: 35% = 0.35.

Step 2: 0.35 × 80 = 28 stones.

Worked Example 4: Mean, median, and range

Five Nile flood heights (metres): 4, 9, 6, 5, and 6. Find the mean, median, and range.

Mean = (4 + 9 + 6 + 5 + 6) ÷ 5 = 30 ÷ 5 = 6 m.

Ordered: 4, 5, 6, 6, 9. Median = 6 m.

Range = 9 − 4 = 5 m.

Worked Example 5: Volume, conversion, and BEDMAS

Convert 3500 mm to metres: 3500 ÷ 1000 = 3.5 m.

A stone block is 50 cm × 40 cm × 30 cm. Volume = 50 × 40 × 30 = 60 000 cm³.

BEDMAS: (60 − 20) ÷ 5 × 3² + 6 → 40 ÷ 5 = 8; 3² = 9; 8 × 9 = 72; 72 + 6 = 78.
