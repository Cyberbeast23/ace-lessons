🍎 Math Week 46: Food's Fantastic Voyage: Digestion Detective's Math Lab

Ace, every bite you take goes on a 9-metre journey. Your teeth chop it, your stomach churns it in acid, your small intestine soaks up the good stuff, and trillions of microbes in your large intestine finish the job. Today you are a digestion detective. You'll use 24-hour clock time, ratios, area, unit rates, converting cm per second into m per minute, powers of ten for the pH scale, fractions of a whole, "how many times bigger," percentages, scale diagrams, a two-step equation, mean vs median, unit prices at the grocery store, and percent increase.

Part 1: Learn the Moves

The 4-Step Problem Solver (your digestion detective's checklist):
  1. Read: What is the question actually asking? Underline the numbers and units (m, cm, mm, m², L, mL, g, s, min, h, $, ¢, %).
  2. Plan: Do the units match? Change metres to centimetres or hours to minutes before you answer.
  3. Solve: Do one step at a time and write each step down.
  4. Check: Is my answer reasonable? A small intestine might be 6.5 m long, but not 6.5 km!

Worked Example 1: The 24-hour clock

Doctors and hospitals use the 24-hour clock: after 12:00 noon, add 12 to the hour. So 1:00 p.m. is 13:00 and 6:30 p.m. is 18:30.

To add time, add hours and minutes separately, then trade 60 min for 1 h: 9:50 + 2 h 25 min = 11 h 75 min = 12:15.

Worked Example 2: Changing a rate's units

To change cm per second into m per minute: multiply by 60 (seconds → minutes), then divide by 100 (cm → m).

5 cm/s × 60 = 300 cm/min. 300 ÷ 100 = 3 m/min.

Worked Example 3: Powers of ten and the pH scale

The pH scale measures how acidic something is. Lower = more acidic. Each step down makes it 10 times more acidic.

pH 4 compared with pH 7 is 3 steps: 10 × 10 × 10 = 10³ = 1 000 times more acidic.

Worked Example 4: Fractions of a whole

If parts of a whole are given as fractions, the leftover part is 1 minus the others. Use a common denominator.

A 12-hour trip: 1/3 + 1/4 = 4/12 + 3/12 = 7/12, so 5/12 is left. 5/12 × 12 h = 5 h.

Worked Example 5: Percent of a total, and percent increase

Percent = part ÷ whole × 100. 3 out of 4 = 0.75 = 75%.

Percent increase = (new − old) ÷ old × 100. From 40 to 50: 10 ÷ 40 = 0.25 = 25% increase.

Worked Example 6: Mean vs median

The mean is the total ÷ how many. The median is the middle value once you put the numbers in order.

4, 10, 7: mean = 21 ÷ 3 = 7. In order 4, 7, 10, so the median is also 7. One very big number pulls the mean up, but barely moves the median.

Worked Example 7: Unit price

To find the better buy, find the price for the same amount of each, like the price per 100 g.

$3.00 for 500 g: 500 g is 5 groups of 100 g, so $3.00 ÷ 5 = $0.60 per 100 g (60¢).

Worked Example 8: Scale diagrams

A scale of 1 : 10 means 1 cm on the diagram stands for 10 cm in real life. To shrink a real length onto paper, divide by 10.

A real 80 cm bone at 1 : 10 is 80 ÷ 10 = 8 cm on paper. (Change metres to centimetres first!)
