Today, while your other lessons explore the giant tides of the Bay of Fundy, you'll learn words for tides and the seashore: tide, high tide, low tide, waves, beach, seashell, crab and sea star. You already know 海 (hoi2, sea), 月亮 (jyut6 loeng6, the Moon), 沙 (saa1, sand), 石頭 (sek6 tau4, rock) and 魚 (jyu4, fish), so you can describe a whole day at the beach! We use Traditional Chinese characters and Jyutping with tone numbers, the Hong Kong way.

How to read Jyutping (sounds):
  • Letters show the sound. Numbers show the tone (the musical pitch): 1 high, 2 rising, 3 mid, 4 low/falling, 5 low rising, 6 low.
  • Example: 沙 saa1 (in 沙灘) is tone 1, high and flat, but 潮 ciu4 is tone 4, low and falling. 蟹 haai5 is tone 5, low and rising. Listen for the difference!
  • Listening/speaking tip: Say each word out loud three times while looking at the characters. Tap Hear model, then Record and compare!

Tide & seashore tips for Ace:
  • 潮水 ciu4 seoi2 = tide (literally "tide water"). 水 is the same 水 you already know from 飲水 (drink water)!
  • 漲 zoeng3 = to rise and 退 teoi3 = to go back. So 漲潮 = the tide comes in (high tide) and 退潮 = the tide goes out (low tide).
  • 海 hoi2 = sea. Put it in front of other words: 海浪 (sea + wave = ocean waves) and 海星 (sea + star = sea star)! You already know 星星 (sing1 sing1, stars).
  • 沙灘 saa1 taan1 = beach (sand + shore). Hong Kong has hundreds of beaches, like 淺水灣 (cin2 seoi2 waan1, Repulse Bay).
  • 貝殼 bui3 hok3 = seashell, and 蟹 haai5 = crab. Crabs are a favourite food in Hong Kong: 大閘蟹 (daai6 zaap6 haai5) is a famous autumn treat!
