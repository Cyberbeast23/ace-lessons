✍️ Writing Week 19: Argument Essay: What Wiped Out the Mammoths? 🦣🧊

For the past four days you've written problem-solution essays. Today, Ace, you'll level up to an argument essay, the kind scientists, lawyers, and judges write. An argument essay takes a clear position (your claim) on a question that people really disagree about, backs it with reasons and evidence, and then does something brave: it explains the other side (the counterclaim) and shows why your claim is still stronger (the rebuttal). Today's big question comes straight from your reading: why did the woolly mammoths and other Ice Age giants disappear?

Part 1: The Lesson

An argument essay has six parts 🧩

Think of it like a scientist presenting a case to other scientists who might not agree yet:

1. Hook + claim: grab attention, then state your position in one clear sentence
2. Background: the facts a reader needs to understand the debate
3. Reason 1 + evidence: your first reason, backed by a fact or example
4. Reason 2 + evidence: a second, different reason with its own evidence
5. Counterclaim + rebuttal: "Some people argue… but…"
6. Conclusion: restate the claim in new words and say why it matters

Five arguer's moves

  • Make a claim someone could disagree with: "Mammoths were big" is a fact, not a claim. "Human hunters were the main reason mammoths vanished" is a claim.
  • Separate reasons from evidence: the reason is why you believe it; the evidence is the fact that proves it (a date, a measurement, a discovery).
  • Use signal words: First, Second, In addition, For example, According to scientists, This shows that…
  • Be fair to the other side: explain the counterclaim honestly, then rebut it with evidence, not insults.
  • Use careful, scientific language: "scientists think," "the evidence suggests," and "probably" are honest when the answer isn't 100% certain.

Grown-up words: a claim is your position; evidence is a fact or example that supports it; a counterclaim is the opposing view; a rebuttal is your answer to it, explaining why your claim still holds.

Model Argument Essay (short version)

Hook + claim + backgroundReasons + evidenceCounterclaim + rebuttalConclusion

Picture a hairy giant taller than an elephant, sweeping snow aside with its tusks; now picture every last one of them gone. About 13 000 to 11 000 years ago, as the Ice Age ended, most of North America's giant mammals disappeared. I believe a warming climate and human hunters worked together to wipe out the woolly mammoth.
First, as the world warmed, the cold grasslands that mammoths grazed turned into forests and wetlands, so their food supply shrank. Second, people armed with stone-tipped spears arrived at about the same time. Because a mammoth mother raised only one calf every few years, herds could not replace the animals that were hunted.
Some people argue that climate change alone was to blame. However, mammoths had already survived earlier warm periods between glaciations, before people arrived, so warming by itself probably was not enough.
In the end, hungry, shrinking herds plus skilled hunters were too much for the mammoths. Their story reminds us that when the climate changes and people put extra pressure on animals, even giants can vanish.

Notice: the model states a clear claim, gives two different reasons with evidence (lost grasslands; hunting and slow births), treats the opposing view fairly before rebutting it with evidence (mammoths survived earlier warm periods), and ends by explaining why the argument matters today.
