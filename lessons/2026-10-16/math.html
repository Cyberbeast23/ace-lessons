🦣 Math Week 19: The Ice Age & Its Giant Mammals Lab

Ace, about 20 000 years ago almost all of British Columbia was buried under a sheet of ice more than a kilometre thick. Woolly mammoths, mastodons, giant ground sloths, and sabre-toothed cats roamed the cold lands south and north of the ice. Today scientists called palaeontologists and glaciologists use math to measure tusks, track how fast glaciers move, read layers in ice cores, and work out how long ago an animal died. You'll level up with scale factors, scale drawings and area, rates (m per day and m per year), negative temperatures and their means, half-life (repeated halving), huge numbers and powers of ten, percents, unit conversions, and reading a line graph with values below zero.

Part 1: Learn the Moves

The 4-Step Problem Solver (your Ice Age scientist's checklist):
  1. Read: What is the question actually asking? Underline the numbers and units.
  2. Plan: Which operations do I need, and in what order?
  3. Solve: Do one step at a time and write each step down.
  4. Check: Is my answer reasonable? Did I answer the question that was asked, in the right unit?

Worked Example 1: Scale factor (mammoth vs elephant)

A scale factor tells you how many times as big one thing is than another. Divide: big ÷ small.

A mammoth 3.5 m tall at the shoulder compared with an elephant 2.8 m tall: 3.5 ÷ 2.8 = 1.25. The mammoth is 1.25 times as tall (25% taller).

In a 1 : 20 museum model, every real length is divided by 20. A 3 m tusk becomes 300 cm ÷ 20 = 15 cm.

Worked Example 2: Scale and area

If every length in a drawing is doubled to get the real object, the area is not doubled. It is multiplied by 2 × 2 = 4.

A drawn footprint 4 cm × 3 cm (12 cm²) at scale 1 : 2 is really 8 cm × 6 cm = 48 cm², which is 4 × 12.

Worked Example 3: Rates (how fast does a glacier move?)

Distance = rate × time. Time = distance ÷ rate. Rate = distance ÷ time.

A glacier creeping 0.2 m per day moves 0.2 × 365 = 73 m per year.

To travel 3 km = 3000 m at 50 m per year takes 3000 ÷ 50 = 60 years. Always make the units match first!

Worked Example 4: Negative temperatures

The difference between −30 °C and −8 °C is −8 − (−30) = −8 + 30 = 22 °C.

The mean of −10, −4, and 2: add them (−10 + −4 + 2 = −12), then divide by 3 to get −4 °C.

Worked Example 5: Half-life (how scientists date bones)

Living things contain a tiny amount of carbon-14. After an animal dies, half of its carbon-14 disappears about every 5730 years. That time is called a half-life.

Start with 1, then 1/2 after one half-life, 1/4 after two, 1/8 after three. A bone with 1/8 left is about 3 × 5730 = 17 190 years old.

Big numbers: 2 600 000 = 2.6 × 1 000 000 = 2.6 × 106 (the 6 counts the places the decimal point moves).
