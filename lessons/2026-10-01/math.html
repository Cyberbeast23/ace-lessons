🧮 Math Week 4: Dinosaur Dig Math Lab

Today we leave the clinic, Ace, and dig into the Mesozoic Era! You'll use fractions, ratios, percentages, BEDMAS, mean/median/range, unit rates, area/volume, scale, and elapsed time — just like a palaeontologist measuring fossils, dig sites, and museum halls.

Part 1: Learn the Moves

The 4-Step Problem Solver (your dig-site check-up):
  1. Read: What is the question actually asking? Underline the numbers and units.
  2. Plan: Which operations do I need, and in what order?
  3. Solve: Do one step at a time and write each step down.
  4. Check: Is my answer reasonable? Did I answer the question that was asked?

Worked Example 1: Scale model length

A museum model of a T. rex is built at a scale of 1 : 20. The model is 65 cm long. How long was the real dinosaur in centimetres?

Step 1: Real length = model × scale factor = 65 × 20 = 1300 cm.

Check: 1300 ÷ 20 = 65 ✔

Worked Example 2: Fractions of fossils found

A dig site has 48 fossil pieces. Ace's team has cleaned 3/8 of them. How many pieces are cleaned?

Step 1: 48 ÷ 8 = 6; 6 × 3 = 18 pieces.

Worked Example 3: Percent of herbivores

Out of 25 dinosaurs on a field chart, 40% are herbivores. How many are herbivores?

Step 1: 40% = 0.40.

Step 2: 0.40 × 25 = 10 herbivores.

Worked Example 4: Mean, median, and range

Five fossil dig depths (metres): 2, 9, 5, 4, and 5. Find the mean, median, and range.

Mean = (2 + 9 + 5 + 4 + 5) ÷ 5 = 25 ÷ 5 = 5 m.

Ordered: 2, 4, 5, 5, 9. Median = 5 m.

Range = 9 − 2 = 7 m.

Worked Example 5: Volume, conversion, and BEDMAS

Convert 2500 mm to metres: 2500 ÷ 1000 = 2.5 m.

A fossil crate is 40 cm × 30 cm × 20 cm. Volume = 40 × 30 × 20 = 24 000 cm³.

BEDMAS: (50 − 10) ÷ 5 × 3² + 4 → 40 ÷ 5 = 8; 3² = 9; 8 × 9 = 72; 72 + 4 = 76.
