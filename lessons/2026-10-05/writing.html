✍️ Writing Week 8: Narrative Writing (A Mission in Space)

Yesterday you persuaded and explained. Today, Ace, you'll tell a story: a first-person narrative about a moment in space, with a strong hook, vivid sensory details, a little dialogue, a problem that builds tension, and an ending that shows how the character changed.

Part 1: The Lesson

Narrative = a story with a shape

A strong narrative doesn't just list events ("then... then... then..."). It pulls the reader into a moment. Good writers show instead of only telling: instead of "I was scared," they write "My hands shook so hard my glove tapped against the hatch."

Hook: start in the action or with a surprise
Setting: where and when, using the senses
Problem: something goes wrong or gets hard
Dialogue: a few real-sounding lines
Resolution: how it ends and what changed

Useful time-order and suspense words: suddenly, at first, a moment later, meanwhile, just as, finally, without warning, by the time.

Dialogue rules: Put the spoken words inside quotation marks. Put the comma or end mark inside the closing quotation mark. Start a new paragraph each time a new person speaks. Example: "Check your oxygen," said Commander Lee.

Model Narrative Paragraph

HookSetting / sensory detailsProblem + dialogueResolution

The warning light blinked red just as I floated out of the airlock. Below my boots, Earth turned slowly, a swirl of blue ocean and white cloud, so bright it made my eyes water inside the helmet. The only sounds were my own breathing and the soft hum of the fans in my suit. "Ace, your tether clip is loose," crackled Commander Lee's voice over the radio. My heart thumped against my ribs. I reached for the clip, but my thick glove slid right off it. "Slow down," she said calmly. "Breathe first, then try again." I took one long breath, counted to three, and pressed the clip until I felt it snap shut. Back inside the station, I stared out the window at the planet I had almost drifted away from, and I knew I would never rush a safety check again.

Notice: The story starts in the action, uses sight and sound, builds a problem with dialogue, and ends by showing what the character learned.
