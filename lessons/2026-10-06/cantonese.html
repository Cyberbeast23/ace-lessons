🗣️ Cantonese Week 8: Big Numbers 11–100 數字 (Hong Kong)

Ace, you already know the numbers 一 to 十 (1–10), plus greetings, family, food, school, body parts, colours, and sky & space words. Today — while your maths lesson cracks Roman numerals — you'll build bigger numbers up to 100 the Hong Kong way, using Traditional Chinese characters and Jyutping with tone numbers. Good news: Cantonese numbers are like LEGO. Once you know 1 to 10, you can snap together every number up to 99!

How to read Jyutping (sounds):
  • Letters show the sound. Numbers show the tone (the musical pitch): 1 high, 2 rising, 3 mid, 4 low/falling, 5 low rising, 6 low.
  • Example: sap6 in 十 (ten) — say sap with tone 6 (low and level).
  • Listening/speaking tip: Say each number out loud three times while looking at the characters. Tap Hear model, then Record and compare!

Number-building tip for Ace:
  • 十 + a number = the teens: 十 + 一 = 十一 (11), 十 + 五 = 十五 (15).
  • a number + 十 = the tens: 二 + 十 = 二十 (20), 三 + 十 = 三十 (30). It means "two tens," "three tens."
  • number + 十 + number = everything in between: 二十三 = 2 tens + 3 = 23. 九十九 = 9 tens + 9 = 99.
  • Compare with Rome: Romans wrote 23 as XXIII (10 + 10 + 1 + 1 + 1). Cantonese says 二十三 — "two-ten-three." 一百 (100) is the Roman C!
  • Fun fact: in fast, everyday speech, people in Hong Kong often shorten the twenties to 廿 jaa6, so 二十三 can sound like 廿三 jaa6 saam1. You don't need to learn it yet — just listen for it!
