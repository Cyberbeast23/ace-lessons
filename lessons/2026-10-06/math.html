🏛️ Math Week 9: Ancient Rome Math Lab

Salve, Ace! (That's Latin for "hello.") Today you'll march through Ancient Rome with Roman numerals with big values, BCE/CE timelines, slope and percent grade (aqueducts!), flow rates and unit conversions, Roman miles to kilometres, percent of a total, percent increase, two-step equations, circle measurement, and ratios — the same maths Roman engineers needed to move water, build roads, and pack 50 000 fans into the Colosseum.

Part 1: Learn the Moves

The 4-Step Problem Solver (your legion's battle plan):
  1. Read: What is the question actually asking? Underline the numbers and units.
  2. Plan: Which operations do I need, and in what order?
  3. Solve: Do one step at a time and write each step down.
  4. Check: Is my answer reasonable? Did I answer the question that was asked?

Worked Example 1: Reading big Roman numerals

The seven symbols: I = 1, V = 5, X = 10, L = 50, C = 100, D = 500, M = 1000.

Rule 1: Big to small means add: VI = 5 + 1 = 6.

Rule 2: A smaller symbol in front of a bigger one means subtract. Only six pairs are allowed: IV = 4, IX = 9, XL = 40, XC = 90, CD = 400, CM = 900.

Example: MCMLXXIV → M (1000) + CM (900) + L (50) + XX (20) + IV (4) = 1974.

Going the other way, split the number by place value: 2026 = 2000 + 20 + 6 = MM + XX + VI = MMXXVI.

Worked Example 2: BCE and CE timelines

BCE years count down toward year 1; CE years count up. There is no year 0: 1 BCE is followed directly by 1 CE.

Both BCE: from 300 BCE to 100 BCE is 300 − 100 = 200 years.

Crossing from BCE to CE: from 50 BCE to 50 CE is 50 + 50 − 1 = 99 years (subtract 1 because year 0 never happened).

Worked Example 3: Slope (gradient) of an aqueduct

An aqueduct carried water downhill using only gravity. Suppose a channel drops 8 m over 40 km.

Drop per kilometre = 8 m ÷ 40 km = 0.2 m per km (20 cm every kilometre!).

Percent grade = drop ÷ horizontal distance × 100, with both in the same unit. 40 km = 40 000 m, so 8 ÷ 40 000 × 100 = 0.02%.

Worked Example 4: Flow rates and unit conversions

A fountain pours 2 L per second. How much in one hour?

1 hour = 60 × 60 = 3600 seconds, so 2 × 3600 = 7200 L.

Remember: 1 m³ = 1000 L, so 7200 L = 7.2 m³.

Worked Example 5: Percent increase and two-step equations

Percent increase = increase ÷ starting amount × 100. From 200 to 260: increase 60, so 60 ÷ 200 × 100 = 30%.

Two-step equation: 6n + 20 = 110 → undo the + 20 first: 6n = 90 → undo the × 6: n = 15.

Typing Roman numerals: Some questions ask you to write a Roman numeral. Type capital or small letters with no spaces, like MMXXVI.
