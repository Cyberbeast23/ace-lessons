Today, while your other lessons explore the giant reptiles that swam in BC's ancient seas, you'll learn words for describing prehistoric animals: dinosaur, reptile, dolphin, neck, very long, tail, bone, and deep. You already know 化石 (faa3 sek6, fossil), 海 (hoi2, sea), 博物館 (bok3 mat6 gun2, museum) and 游水 (jau4 seoi2, swim), so listen for them in today's sentences. We use Traditional Chinese characters and Jyutping with tone numbers, the Hong Kong way.

How to read Jyutping (sounds):
  • Letters show the sound. Numbers show the tone (the musical pitch): 1 high, 2 rising, 3 mid, 4 low/falling, 5 low rising, 6 low.
  • Example: 頸 geng2 (neck) is tone 2, rising up like a question, while yesterday's 驚 geng1 (scared) is tone 1, high and flat. Same sounds, different tone, totally different word! 深 sam1 (deep) sounds exactly like 心 sam1 (heart), which you learned on heart day.
  • Listening/speaking tip: Say each word out loud three times while looking at the characters. Tap Hear model, then Record and compare!

Sea reptile tips for Ace:
  • 恐龍 hung2 lung4 = dinosaur, literally "scary dragon." 恐 = fear, 龍 = dragon. Many prehistoric reptile names end in 龍 even when they are not dinosaurs!
  • 魚龍 (jyu4 lung4, "fish dragon") = ichthyosaur. 蛇頸龍 (se4 geng2 lung4, "snake-neck dragon") = plesiosaur. 滄龍 (cong1 lung4, "sea dragon") = mosasaur. Bonus words: you don't need to memorize them, but listen for 頸 in 蛇頸龍!
  • 爬蟲 paa4 cung4 = reptile, literally "crawling creature." 爬 = crawl or climb. 蟲 (cung4) is the word you learned for bug.
  • Body parts use a counter word: 條 (tiu4) for long, thin things. 條頸 = the neck, 條尾 = the tail. 好長 hou2 coeng4 = very long, just like 好大, 好重 and 好高 that you already know.
  • 骨 gwat1 = bone. 恐龍骨 = dinosaur bones. 深 sam1 = deep. 個海好深 = the sea is very deep.
