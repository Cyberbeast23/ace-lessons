Ace, you already know numbers up to 100, plus greetings, family, food, school, body parts, colours, sky & space words, what to say at the doctor's, nature words, question words, music words, trains & travel, brain & sleep words, bees & gardens, electricity & lights, land, people & respect, ice, snow & giant animals, community, rules & voting, weather & storms, germ-fighter words, ancient China words, and flight words. Today, while your other lessons explore the Cariboo Gold Rush, you'll learn gold rush words: gold, Gold Mountain, sand, to look for, miner, very heavy, money, and to strike it rich. Here's something amazing: thousands of the miners who came to British Columbia in the 1860s spoke Cantonese, the same language you're learning! They called this land 金山 (gam1 saan1), Gold Mountain. You already know 山 (saan1, mountain) and 工人 (gung1 jan4, worker), so listen for them inside today's words! Bonus: San Francisco is still called 舊金山 (gau6 gam1 saan1), "Old Gold Mountain"! We use Traditional Chinese characters and Jyutping with tone numbers, the Hong Kong way.

How to read Jyutping (sounds):
  • Letters show the sound. Numbers show the tone (the musical pitch): 1 high, 2 rising, 3 mid, 4 low/falling, 5 low rising, 6 low.
  • Example: hou2 cung5 in 好重 (very heavy) — 好 is tone 2 (rising, like asking "hou?") and 重 is tone 5 (a low rise, starting low and lifting just a little). Compare with 金 gam1, tone 1: high and flat, as bright as shining gold!
  • Listening/speaking tip: Say each word out loud three times while looking at the characters. Tap Hear model, then Record and compare!

Gold rush tips for Ace:
  • 金 gam1 means gold. It is also part of 金色 (gam1 sik1), the colour gold, and 金魚 (gam1 jyu2), goldfish!
  • 金山 gam1 saan1 = "gold" + "mountain." Cantonese-speaking miners from Guangdong called North America 金山. San Francisco became 舊金山 (gau6 gam1 saan1, Old Gold Mountain).
  • 沙 saa1 means sand. Miners swirled 沙 and water in a pan, and the heavy gold sank to the bottom. 沙灘 (saa1 taan1) is a sandy beach.
  • 搵 wan2 means to look for or to find. It is a very everyday word: 我搵唔到我隻鞋！ (ngo5 wan2 m4 dou2 ngo5 zek3 haai4!) = I can't find my shoe!
  • 礦工 kwong3 gung1 means miner. 礦 (kwong3) = mine and 工 (gung1) = work, the same 工 as in 工人 (worker).
  • 好重 hou2 cung5 = very heavy, 錢 cin2 = money, and 發達 faat3 daat6 = to strike it rich. A miner's dream: 搵到金，發達喇！ (wan2 dou2 gam1, faat3 daat6 laa3!) = Found gold, I'm rich! 💰
