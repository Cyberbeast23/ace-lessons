🚒 Math Week 43: Ready to Respond: Fire Captain's Math Lab

Ace, when the alarm sounds at a fire hall, firefighters have about a minute to pull on their gear and roll out the door — and every decision after that uses math. How fast can the engine get there? How much water is left in the tank? How long will a firefighter's air last? Today you are the captain of an imaginary fire hall, Station 7, using distance–speed–time in minutes, water flow rates, tank capacity, ladder lengths with overlaps, crew totals, temperature differences with negative numbers, percent of homes with smoke alarms, the median of response times, percent from a bar graph, the area of an L-shaped floor plan, room volume and ventilation, a two-step equation, air-supply safety limits, GST + PST on safety gear, and a floor-plan scale.

Part 1: Learn the Moves

The 4-Step Problem Solver (your fire captain's checklist):
  1. Read: What is the question actually asking? Underline the numbers and units (m, cm, km, km/h, min, L, L/min, °C, m², m³, kg, $, %).
  2. Plan: Do the units match? Change metres to centimetres or hours to minutes before you answer.
  3. Solve: Do one step at a time and write each step down.
  4. Check: Is my answer reasonable? A fire engine might drive at 56 km/h in the city, but not 5 600 km/h!

Worked Example 1: Travel time in minutes

Time = distance ÷ speed gives hours when speed is in km/h. Multiply hours by 60 to get minutes.

3 km at 40 km/h: 3 ÷ 40 = 0.075 h. 0.075 × 60 = 4.5 minutes.

Worked Example 2: Flow rates

A flow rate like 500 L/min tells how many litres pass each minute. Total = rate × time and time = total ÷ rate.

500 L/min for 6 min = 3 000 L. A 2 000 L tank at 250 L/min lasts 2 000 ÷ 250 = 8 min.

Worked Example 3: Differences with negative numbers

The difference between a high and a low temperature is high − low. Subtracting a negative is the same as adding.

From −8 °C to 25 °C: 25 − (−8) = 25 + 8 = 33 °C.

Worked Example 4: The median

The median is the middle value after you put the data in order. With an odd number of values there is exactly one middle.

7, 3, 9, 4, 6 → 3, 4, 6, 7, 9. Median = 6.

Worked Example 5: Area of an L-shape

Find the area of the big rectangle, then subtract the missing corner (or split the shape into two rectangles and add).

10 m × 8 m = 80 m². Missing corner 3 m × 2 m = 6 m². 80 − 6 = 74 m².

Worked Example 6: Percent of a whole and "what's left"

If 90% do something, then 100% − 90% = 10% do not. Change the percent to a decimal and multiply.

10% of 400 = 0.10 × 400 = 40.

Worked Example 7: Two taxes at once

In British Columbia many items have 5% GST + 7% PST = 12% tax. Multiply the subtotal by 1.12, then round to the nearest cent.

$50.00 × 1.12 = $56.00.

Worked Example 8: Scale drawings

A scale of 1 : 50 means 1 cm on the plan is 50 cm in real life. Multiply, then change units (100 cm = 1 m).

6 cm on the plan × 50 = 300 cm = 3 m.
