You already know 皇帝 (wong4 dai3, emperor), 國王 (gwok3 wong4, king), 山 (saan1, mountain), 石頭 (sek6 tau4, stone) and 橋 (kiu4, bridge), so you can tell the story of an ancient mountain empire! We use Traditional Chinese characters and Jyutping with tone numbers, the Hong Kong way.

How to read Jyutping (sounds):
  • Letters show the sound. Numbers show the tone (the musical pitch): 1 high, 2 rising, 3 mid, 4 low/falling, 5 low rising, 6 low.
  • Example: in 梯田, 梯 tai1 is tone 1, high and flat, but 田 tin4 is tone 4, low and falling. 代 doi6 is tone 6, low and level, 帝 dai3 is tone 3, in the middle, and 史 si2 is tone 2, rising from the middle. Listen for the difference!
  • Listening/speaking tip: Say each word out loud three times while looking at the characters. Tap Hear model, then Record and compare!

History & mountain tips for Ace:
  • 帝國 dai3 gwok3 = empire. You know 皇帝 (wong4 dai3, emperor) and 國王 (gwok3 wong4, king). 帝 = emperor and 國 = country, so an empire is an "emperor's country"! The Inca Empire is 印加帝國 jan3 gaa1 dai3 gwok3.
  • 歷史 lik6 si2 (history) and 古代 gu2 doi6 (ancient times) go together: 古代歷史 gu2 doi6 lik6 si2 = ancient history.
  • 梯田 tai1 tin4: 梯 = ladder/stairs and 田 = field, so terraced fields are "staircase fields," exactly what the Inca built on their mountains! (Bonus: 樓梯 lau4 tai1 means stairs.)
  • 薯仔 syu4 zai2 is the Hong Kong word for potato. Now you can guess 薯條 syu4 tiu2: French fries! 🍟
  • 羊駝 joeng4 to4 = alpaca (or llama): 羊 = sheep and 駝 = camel. An alpaca really does look like a woolly sheep-camel!
