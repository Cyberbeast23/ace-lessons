✍️ Writing Week 1: Building a Strong Paragraph

Doctors write every day: patient notes, reports, and research papers. Their writing has to be clear, organized, and backed by evidence. This week, Ace, you'll learn how to build a paragraph like an expert.

Part 1: The Lesson

The Paragraph Burger 🍔

A strong informational paragraph is built like a burger. The buns hold everything together, and the fillings are the good stuff in the middle.

Top bun: Topic sentence. Tells the reader the main idea. It should be clear and interesting, and not too specific.
Filling: Evidence / supporting details. Facts, examples, or numbers that prove or support the main idea. Strong paragraphs have 2 or 3.
Filling: Explanation. Tell the reader why the evidence matters or how it connects to the main idea. Use linking words: because, this means, as a result, for example, in addition, however.
Bottom bun: Concluding sentence. Wraps up by restating the main idea in new words, or ends with a final thought. Don't just copy the topic sentence!

Model Paragraph

Topic sentenceEvidenceExplanationConclusion

Your bones are not dry, dead sticks. They are living organs that are constantly changing. For example, when a bone breaks, special cells rush to the injury and begin building new bone tissue within days. This means a broken arm can actually heal itself, as long as a doctor sets it straight and a cast holds it still. In addition, a baby is born with around 300 bones, but many of them slowly fuse together until an adult has only 206. Something that can grow, repair itself, and change shape over time must be alive. The next time you look at a skeleton, remember that the bones inside you are busy, growing, living parts of your body.

Notice: Every sentence is about the same main idea (bones are alive). A sentence like "I like watching hockey" would not belong, even if it's true!
