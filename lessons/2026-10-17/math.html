🗳️ Math Week 20: How Canada's Government Works Lab

Ace, Canada has three main levels of government: the federal government in Ottawa (Parliament), the provincial government in Victoria (the Legislative Assembly of BC), and your municipal government (a city or town council led by a mayor). Every one of them runs on numbers: votes are counted, seats are added up, majorities are calculated, and budgets worth millions of dollars are shared out. Today you'll think like an election officer and a town council treasurer with percentages and vote shares, voter turnout, majority and two-thirds thresholds, ratios, mean and median, budgets, percent increase, tax rates, big-number division, and reading a bar graph.

Part 1: Learn the Moves

The 4-Step Problem Solver (your election officer's checklist):
  1. Read: What is the question actually asking? Underline the numbers and units.
  2. Plan: Which operations do I need, and in what order?
  3. Solve: Do one step at a time and write each step down.
  4. Check: Is my answer reasonable? Did I answer the question that was asked?

Worked Example 1: Vote share as a percent

Percent = part ÷ whole × 100.

In a class election, 30 students vote and Sam gets 12 votes: 12 ÷ 30 = 0.4 = 40% of the vote.

Voter turnout is the percent of people who were allowed to vote that actually did. If 8000 people could vote and 5000 did: 5000 ÷ 8000 = 0.625 = 62.5%.

Worked Example 2: What counts as a majority?

A majority means more than half. Exactly half is a tie, not a majority!

With 9 council members: half is 4.5, so a majority is the next whole number, 5. With 10 members: half is 5, so a majority is 6.

Some big decisions need a two-thirds vote. With 12 members: 2/3 × 12 = 8 yes votes are needed. If 2/3 is not a whole number, round up.

Worked Example 3: Ratios

If council votes 6 in favour and 3 against, the ratio for : against is 6 : 3, which simplifies to 2 : 1 (divide both by 3).

A ratio of 2 : 1 means 2 out of every 3 votes are "for." In a group of 30 voting the same way: 2/3 × 30 = 20 for and 10 against.

Worked Example 4: Mean and median

The mean is the total ÷ how many. Mean of 40, 50, 60, 70 = 220 ÷ 4 = 55.

The median is the middle value once the numbers are sorted. With an even count, it is halfway between the two middle numbers: for 3, 8, 10, 15 the median is (8 + 10) ÷ 2 = 9.

Worked Example 5: Budgets and percent change

A town spends 20% of a $50 000 000 budget on roads: 0.20 × 50 000 000 = $10 000 000.

Percent increase = (new − old) ÷ old × 100. Going from 400 votes to 500 votes: 100 ÷ 400 = 0.25 = 25% more.

A tax rate "per $1000" means: divide the value by 1000, then multiply by the rate. $200 000 at $4 per $1000: 200 × 4 = $800.
