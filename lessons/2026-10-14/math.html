⚡ Math Week 17: Electricity & Circuits Lab

Ace, electricity is the invisible river that powers lights, laptops, and whole cities. Engineers track volts, amps, and watts, compare series vs parallel circuits, and convert units like milliamps and kilowatt-hours. Today you'll think like a future electrical engineer. You'll level up with power (P = V × I), series and parallel bulb counts, mA↔A and kWh conversions, resistance ranking, multi-step word problems, and reading a household power bar graph.

Part 1: Learn the Moves

The 4-Step Problem Solver (your electrician's checklist):
  1. Read: What is the question actually asking? Underline the numbers and units.
  2. Plan: Which operations do I need, and in what order?
  3. Solve: Do one step at a time and write each step down.
  4. Check: Is my answer reasonable? Did I answer the question that was asked, in the right unit?

Worked Example 1: Power (watts = volts × amps)

Electric power P (in watts, W) = voltage V (volts) × current I (amperes).

A kettle draws 10 A from a 120 V outlet: P = 120 × 10 = 1200 W.

If you know power and voltage, current = power ÷ voltage. A 60 W bulb on 120 V draws 60 ÷ 120 = 0.5 A.

Worked Example 2: Series vs parallel bulbs

In a series circuit, bulbs share one path. If one burns out, the whole string goes dark.

In a parallel circuit, each bulb has its own path. One burnt bulb does not turn the others off.

Three identical 2 V cells in series give 6 V total. Two identical 1.5 V cells in parallel still give 1.5 V (but last longer).

Worked Example 3: Unit conversions (mA and A)

1 ampere (A) = 1000 milliamperes (mA). A phone charger at 500 mA = 0.5 A.

Energy in kilowatt-hours: 1 kWh = 1000 W used for 1 hour. A 100 W lamp for 5 hours uses 500 Wh = 0.5 kWh.

Worked Example 4: Resistance ranking

Higher resistance means less current for the same voltage (Ohm's idea: I = V ÷ R).

At 12 V, a 4 Ω resistor draws 12 ÷ 4 = 3 A; a 6 Ω resistor draws only 2 A. The 6 Ω part has more resistance.

Worked Example 5: Multi-step circuit cost

A heater uses 1500 W for 2 hours: energy = 1500 × 2 = 3000 Wh = 3 kWh.

If electricity costs $0.12 per kWh: cost = 3 × 0.12 = $0.36.
