🏒 Math Week 52: Hockey by the Numbers: Team Statistician's Math Lab

Ace, every NHL team, every junior team and even many minor hockey clubs now have someone whose job is to turn the game into numbers: a statistician (stat-ih-STISH-un). They count shots, saves, goals, passes and seconds of ice time, then use math to answer real questions: Which goalie is really stopping more pucks? Is our power play getting better? How fast does a slapshot fly? Today you are the statistician for the Riverside Rapids, a made-up Peewee team. You'll use area of a rectangle, percents and rates (shooting percentage and save percentage), goals-against average, converting m/s to km/h, distance = speed × time, the team standings formula, ratios as percents, integers (plus/minus), median vs. mean, the probability of two events, the volume of a cylinder, very thin layers in millimetres, a one-step algebra equation, and a "how many more do we need?" percent problem.

Part 1: Learn the Moves

The 4-Step Problem Solver (your team statistician's checklist):
  1. Read: What is the question actually asking? Underline the numbers and units (m, cm, mm, m², cm³, L, m/s, km/h, seconds, minutes, %).
  2. Plan: Do the units match? Change litres to cubic metres or seconds to minutes before you answer.
  3. Solve: Do one step at a time and write each step down.
  4. Check: Is my answer reasonable? A save percentage can't be more than 100%, and a puck doesn't fly 5 000 km/h!

Worked Example 1: Area of the ice

A practice rink is 50 m long and 25 m wide. If we pretend the rounded corners are square, its area is 50 × 25 = 1 250 m².

Worked Example 2: Shooting percentage

Shooting percentage = goals ÷ shots on goal × 100. A player with 6 goals on 40 shots: 6 ÷ 40 = 0.15, so 15%. That means about 15 of every 100 shots go in.

Worked Example 3: Save percentage

A goalie faces 25 shots and lets in 2 goals, so she makes 25 − 2 = 23 saves. Save percentage = saves ÷ shots × 100 = 23 ÷ 25 × 100 = 92%. (The NHL often writes it as a decimal: .920.)

Worked Example 4: Goals-against average (a rate)

Goals-against average (GAA) = goals allowed × 60 ÷ minutes played. It answers: "How many goals would this goalie allow in one full 60-minute game, on average?" 5 goals in 150 minutes: 5 × 60 ÷ 150 = 2.0.

Worked Example 5: m/s to km/h

1 m/s means 1 m every second. An hour has 3 600 seconds, so that's 3 600 m = 3.6 km every hour. To change m/s to km/h, multiply by 3.6. 25 m/s × 3.6 = 90 km/h.

Worked Example 6: Time = distance ÷ speed

A pass travels 12 m at 20 m/s. Time = 12 ÷ 20 = 0.6 seconds.

Worked Example 7: Standings points

In many leagues a win = 2 points, an overtime (OT) loss = 1 point, and a loss in regular time = 0 points. 10 wins, 5 regulation losses and 3 OT losses: 10 × 2 + 3 × 1 = 23 points.

Worked Example 8: A ratio as a percent of the whole

If the shots were 30 for us and 20 for them, the ratio is 30 : 20, and the whole is 30 + 20 = 50 shots. Our share = 30 ÷ 50 = 0.6 = 60%.

Worked Example 9: Integers and plus/minus

A player gets +1 when her team scores while she's on the ice and −1 when the other team scores. On for 9 goals for and 12 against: 9 − 12 = −3.

Worked Example 10: Median vs. mean

Goals in 5 games: 1, 6, 2, 1, 0. Mean = (1 + 6 + 2 + 1 + 0) ÷ 5 = 10 ÷ 5 = 2. Median: put them in order (0, 1, 1, 2, 6) and take the middle one: 1. One big game (6 goals) pulls the mean up, but not the median.

Worked Example 11: Probability of two things both happening

If something happens with probability 1/2 and then (separately) something else with probability 1/4, the chance that both happen is 1/2 × 1/4 = 1/8. Multiply the probabilities.

Worked Example 12: Volume of a cylinder

Volume of a cylinder = area of the circle × height = π × r × r × h. Use π ≈ 3.14. Radius 2 cm, height 5 cm: 3.14 × 2 × 2 × 5 = 62.8 cm³. Remember: the radius is half the diameter!

Worked Example 13: Spreading water very thin

1 m³ = 1 000 L. Spread 500 L (0.5 m³) over 1 000 m²: thickness = volume ÷ area = 0.5 ÷ 1 000 = 0.0005 m = 0.5 mm.

Worked Example 14: Equations with "times as many"

Points = goals + assists. If a player has 2 times as many assists as goals and 18 points: call goals g. Then g + 2g = 18, so 3g = 18 and g = 6 goals (and 12 assists).
