🧭 Math Week 61: Lost in the Ice! Arctic Navigator's Math Lab

Ace, in May 1845, two Royal Navy ships, HMS Erebus and HMS Terror, sailed from England with 129 men under Sir John Franklin. Their mission: find the Northwest Passage, a sea route through the frozen islands of Canada's Arctic. None of them ever came home. For more than 160 years, the ships were lost, until Inuit knowledge and modern sonar finally found them in 2014 and 2016. Today you're an Arctic navigator and search planner. You'll use negative temperatures, counting days across a leap year, percents, map scales, rates, the volume of a cylinder, the mean, compass bearings, search areas, two-step equations, unit conversions (knots to km/h) and the area of a circle.

Part 1: Learn the Moves

The 4-Step Problem Solver (a navigator's checklist):
  1. Read: What is the question actually asking? Underline the numbers and units (°C, km, m, cm, kg, mL, hours, days, knots).
  2. Plan: Do the units match? Change knots to km/h or cm on a map to km before you calculate.
  3. Solve: Do one step at a time and write each step down.
  4. Check: Is my answer reasonable? Arctic winters are far below zero, and nobody hauls a sledge 100 km in a day!

Worked Example 1: Adding and subtracting negative temperatures

Start at −20 °C. Warming 8 degrees means add: −20 + 8 = −12 °C. Then cooling 15 degrees means subtract: −12 − 15 = −27 °C. Picture a thermometer: up is warmer, down is colder.

Worked Example 2: Counting days between dates (watch for leap years!)

From 20 March to 5 May: 11 days left in March (31 − 20), 30 in April, then 5 in May: 11 + 30 + 5 = 46 days. A leap year (like 1848 or 2028) has 29 days in February instead of 28.

Worked Example 3: Percent of a group

If 12 out of 80 students walk to school, 12 ÷ 80 = 0.15 = 15%. Part ÷ whole, then × 100.

Worked Example 4: Map scales

If 1 cm on a map stands for 20 km, then 7.5 cm stands for 7.5 × 20 = 150 km.

Worked Example 5: Rates: how long will it take?

Time = distance ÷ speed. Walking 60 km at 4 km per day takes 60 ÷ 4 = 15 days.

Worked Example 6: Percent decrease, then share

Remove 20% of a 500 kg load: 500 × 0.80 = 400 kg left. Shared by 8 people: 400 ÷ 8 = 50 kg each.

Worked Example 7: Volume of a cylinder

V = π × r × r × h. A can with radius 4 cm and height 10 cm: 3.14 × 4 × 4 × 10 = 502.4 cm³. Since 1 cm³ = 1 mL, it holds 502.4 mL.

Worked Example 8: The mean of negative numbers

Mean of −10, −14 and −6: add them (−30), then divide by 3: −10. The mean of negatives is still negative.

Worked Example 9: Compass bearings

Bearings are measured clockwise from north: north = 0°, east = 90°, south = 180°, west = 270°. Heading west (270°) and turning 40° toward north means turning clockwise: 270 + 40 = 310°.

Worked Example 10: Area swept by a moving strip

A strip 0.5 km wide moving 6 km covers a rectangle 0.5 × 6 = 3 km². Make sure both lengths are in the same unit (200 m = 0.2 km).

Worked Example 11: Two-step equations

"500 plus 300 times a number is 2 900" means 500 + 300n = 2 900. Undo in reverse order: 2 900 − 500 = 2 400, then 2 400 ÷ 300 = 8.

Worked Example 12: Knots to kilometres per hour

Sailors measure speed in knots. 1 knot = 1.852 km/h, so 10 knots = 18.52 km/h, and in 2 hours a ship travels 18.52 × 2 = 37.04 km.

Worked Example 13: Area of a circle

A = π × r × r. A circle with diameter 4 m has radius 2 m, so A = 3.14 × 2 × 2 = 12.56 m². Don't forget to halve the diameter first!
