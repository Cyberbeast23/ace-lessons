🏺 Math Week 57: Land Between the Rivers! Mesopotamian Scribe's Math Lab

Ace, more than 5 000 years ago, in the land between the Tigris and Euphrates rivers (today mostly Iraq), the Sumerians invented one of the world's first writing systems, and the very first things they wrote down were numbers: sacks of barley, jars of oil and herds of sheep. Their scribes counted in groups of 60, and we still use their system every time we read a clock or measure an angle. Today you're training at a scribe school, the edubba or "tablet house." You'll use base-60 place value, unit conversions with 60s, area of a rectangle, volume of a rectangular prism, rounding down for whole items, multi-step rates, percent of a total, BCE/CE timelines with no year 0, speed-distance-time, fractions of an amount, probability of independent events, two-step equations and map scales.

Part 1: Learn the Moves

The 4-Step Problem Solver (a scribe's checklist):
  1. Read: What is the question actually asking? Underline the numbers and units (cm, m, km, cm³, m³, L, s, h, years).
  2. Plan: Do the units match? Change metres to centimetres or hours to seconds before you calculate.
  3. Solve: Do one step at a time and write each step down (scribes pressed every step into wet clay!).
  4. Check: Is my answer reasonable? A clay tablet isn't 3 m wide, and BCE years count down toward year 1.

Worked Example 1: Base-60 place value

We count in tens: 345 = 3 hundreds + 4 tens + 5 ones. Babylonian scribes counted in sixties. Their places were ones, sixties, and 3 600s (60 × 60). So "1 sixty and 30 ones" = 60 + 30 = 90, and "2 three-thousand-six-hundreds" = 2 × 3 600 = 7 200.

Worked Example 2: Breaking a number into sixties

To write 200 the Babylonian way, ask how many 60s fit: 200 ÷ 60 = 3 remainder 20. So 200 = 3 sixties and 20 ones. Check: 3 × 60 + 20 = 200. ✓

Worked Example 3: Conversions that use 60

1 hour = 60 minutes and 1 minute = 60 seconds, so 1 hour = 60 × 60 = 3 600 seconds. A circle has 360 degrees, and each degree can be split into 60 smaller parts called minutes of arc.

Worked Example 4: Area of a rectangle

Area = length × width. A tablet 7 cm long and 4.5 cm wide has an area of 7 × 4.5 = 31.5 cm².

Worked Example 5: Volume of a rectangular prism

Volume = length × width × height. A box 10 m × 5 m × 2 m holds 10 × 5 × 2 = 100 m³.

Worked Example 6: Rounding down for whole items

1 m³ = 100 cm × 100 cm × 100 cm = 1 000 000 cm³. If a block is 5 000 cm³, then 1 000 000 ÷ 5 000 = 200 blocks. If the answer were 200.7, you could only fit 200 whole blocks, so round down.

Worked Example 7: Multi-step rates

If each worker gets 2 L of oil per month, then 10 workers get 20 L per month, and in 6 months they get 20 × 6 = 120 L.

Worked Example 8: Percent of a total

If 45 of 60 tablets are about trade, that's 45 ÷ 60 = 0.75 = 75%.

Worked Example 9: Timelines: BCE to BCE

BCE years count down toward year 1. From 3000 BCE to 2500 BCE is 3 000 − 2 500 = 500 years. (The bigger BCE number is the older date!)

Worked Example 10: Timelines: BCE to CE

There is no year 0: 1 BCE is followed directly by 1 CE. From 500 BCE to 500 CE is 500 + 500 − 1 = 999 years.

Worked Example 11: Speed, distance and time

Distance = speed × time. Change the time to seconds if the speed is in metres per second. At 2 m/s for 1 hour: 2 × 3 600 = 7 200 m = 7.2 km.

Worked Example 12: Fractions of an amount, step by step

If someone takes 1/4 of 400 L, they get 100 L, leaving 300 L. If then 1/10 of what's left is paid as a tax, that's 30 L, leaving 270 L. Always ask: a fraction of what?

Worked Example 13: Probability of independent events

When things don't affect each other, multiply their probabilities. The chance of a coin landing heads twice in a row is 1/2 × 1/2 = 1/4.

Worked Example 14: Two-step equations

"4 more than 5 times a number is 39" means 5n + 4 = 39. Undo the steps in reverse order: 39 − 4 = 35, then 35 ÷ 5 = 7.

Worked Example 15: Map scales

On a 1 : 1 000 000 map, 1 cm on the map is 1 000 000 cm = 10 000 m = 10 km in real life. So 3.5 cm on the map is 3.5 × 10 = 35 km.
