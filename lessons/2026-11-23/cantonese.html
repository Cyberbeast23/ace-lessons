We use Traditional Chinese characters and Jyutping with tone numbers, the Hong Kong way.

How to read Jyutping (sounds):
  • Letters show the sound. Numbers show the tone (the musical pitch): 1 high, 2 rising, 3 mid, 4 low/falling, 5 low rising, 6 low.
  • Today's words use all six tones! 車 ce1 is tone 1 (high and flat), 板 baan2 is tone 2 (rising), 法 faat3 is tone 3 (middle), 文 man4 is tone 4 (low and falling), 市 si5 is tone 5 (low rising), and 字 zi6 is tone 6 (low and flat). Listen for a tricky pair: 時 si4 (time) and 市 si5 (market, city) have the same sounds, and only the tone tells them apart!
  • Listening/speaking tip: Say each word out loud three times while looking at the characters. Tap Hear model, then Record and compare!

Ancient-city tips for Ace:
  • 文 man4 means writing or culture. 文字 man4 zi6 = writing (a writing system): 文 + 字 (character). 文明 man4 ming4 = civilization: 文 + 明 (bright), so a civilization is a "bright culture."
  • 泥板 nai4 baan2 = clay tablet: 泥 = mud or clay + 板 = board. Mesopotamian scribes wrote on "mud boards"!
  • 法律 faat3 leot6 = law. 守法律 (sau2 faat3 leot6) means to obey the law: 守 = to guard or keep. Hammurabi's laws were carved in stone so everyone could keep them.
  • 車輪 ce1 leon4 = wheel: 車 = vehicle + 輪 = wheel. In everyday Hong Kong speech, people often say 車轆 (ce1 luk1) for a wheel. You learned 車轆 with bike words!
  • 農夫 nung4 fu1 = farmer: 農 = farming + 夫 = man or worker. 城市 sing4 si5 = city: 城 = city wall + 市 = market. An ancient city was a market inside a wall! 時間 si4 gaan3 = time: the Babylonians split time into sixties, so 一分鐘有六十秒 (one minute has sixty seconds).
