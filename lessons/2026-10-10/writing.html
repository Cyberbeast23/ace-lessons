✍️ Writing Week 13: Music Review: Be the Critic 🎧

Yesterday you wrote clear instructions. Today, Ace, you'll write like a music critic. A review (also called a critique) tells readers what a piece of music is like and whether it's worth their time. A great review does two jobs at once: it describes the music so vividly that readers can almost hear it, and it gives an opinion backed by evidence. You'll learn how to use music vocabulary, sensory comparisons, and fair criticism. Then you'll review a piece of music yourself.

Part 1: The Lesson

A music review has five parts 🎼

Readers want to know what the music is, what it sounds like, and whether they should listen:

1. Title + introduction: a catchy title, a hook, and the facts: the piece's name, who wrote or performs it, and its genre
2. Description: what you actually hear: instruments, tempo, dynamics (loud and soft), melody, rhythm, and mood
3. Opinion with evidence: what works best, and why, pointing to specific moments
4. Criticism: a fair, respectful point about what could be better
5. Recommendation + rating: who would enjoy it, and a score such as 4 out of 5 stars

Five critic moves

  • Use music words: tempo (speed), dynamics (loudness), crescendo (getting gradually louder), melody (the tune), rhythm (the pattern of beats), percussion, strings, brass, woodwinds.
  • Show, don't just tell: replace tired words like good, nice and cool with precise adjectives and comparisons. "The cymbals crashed like waves against rocks" beats "the cymbals were good."
  • Back every opinion with evidence: "The ending is thrilling because the tempo speeds up until..." An opinion without a reason is just a guess.
  • Be fair: even a five-star piece can have a weak moment. A trustworthy critic is honest and respectful: criticize the music, never the people.
  • Format titles correctly: put the names of songs and short pieces in quotation marks ("Northern Lights") and the names of albums, operas and long works in italics (or underline them when writing by hand).

Grown-up words: a subjective statement is an opinion ("the melody is beautiful"); an objective statement is a fact anyone can check ("the piece lasts about two and a half minutes"). Strong reviews mix both.

Review: A Sneaky Song That Explodes
What if a piece of music could tiptoe and then stampede? "In the Hall of the Mountain King," written by the Norwegian composer Edvard Grieg in the 1870s, is an orchestral piece that does exactly that in under three minutes.
It begins with low, sneaky notes in the bassoons and cellos, like trolls creeping through a dark cave. The same short melody repeats again and again, but each time the tempo gets a little faster and the dynamics grow louder. By the end, the whole orchestra is racing, and the crashing cymbals and pounding drums sound like an avalanche.
The best part is the enormous crescendo, because you can feel the tension building in your chest. However, the melody repeats so many times that the middle can feel slightly predictable.
Overall, I give it four and a half stars out of five. I recommend it to anyone who thinks orchestra music is boring, especially if you like suspenseful movie soundtracks.

Notice: The introduction gives facts (title in quotation marks, composer, genre, length), the description uses music words (tempo, dynamics, crescendo) and a simile, every opinion has a because, and the criticism is fair and specific.
