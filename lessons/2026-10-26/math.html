🚑 Math Week 29: Emergency Room Math Lab

Ace, when someone calls 9-1-1, a whole team swings into action: a dispatcher answers the call, paramedics race out in an ambulance, and an emergency room (ER) team gets ready at the hospital. Every one of them uses math, fast and accurately. Paramedics count heartbeats and breaths, work out how long the oxygen will last, and keep an eye on the clock, because in emergency medicine, minutes matter. Today you'll work like an emergency crew with percents (including percent change), speed-distance-time, rates per minute and per hour, ratios, median of an even set, decimal temperature changes, fractions of a supply, reading a bar graph, elapsed time, comparing travel times, expected results, a two-condition puzzle, and an equation.

Part 1: Learn the Moves

The 4-Step Problem Solver (your paramedic checklist):
  1. Read: What is the question actually asking? Underline the numbers and units.
  2. Plan: Which operations do I need, and in what order? Do the units match (seconds with seconds, minutes with minutes)?
  3. Solve: Do one step at a time and write each step down.
  4. Check: Is my answer reasonable? Did I answer the question that was asked, in the right units?

Worked Example 1: Rates from a short count

Nobody waits a whole minute to count a pulse in an emergency. A paramedic counts beats for a short time and scales up. 18 beats in 15 seconds: there are 4 fifteen-second chunks in a minute, so 18 × 4 = 72 beats per minute.

To go from "per minute" to "per hour," multiply by 60: 12 breaths per minute × 60 = 720 breaths per hour.

Worked Example 2: Speed, distance and time

time = distance ÷ speed. An ambulance drives 10 km at 60 km/h: 10 ÷ 60 = 1/6 of an hour. Change hours to minutes by multiplying by 60: 1/6 × 60 = 10 minutes.

Worked Example 3: Percent and percent change

What percent? Part ÷ whole × 100. If 45 out of 50 calls were answered quickly: 45 ÷ 50 = 0.9, and 0.9 × 100 = 90%.

Percent increase = (change ÷ original) × 100. From 200 patients to 230 patients, the change is 30. 30 ÷ 200 = 0.15 = 15% increase. Always divide by the starting amount!

Worked Example 4: Ratios in groups

A pattern of 3 red cards then 1 blue card repeats. Each group has 3 + 1 = 4 cards. In 20 cards there are 20 ÷ 4 = 5 groups, so there are 5 × 1 = 5 blue cards.

Worked Example 5: Median of an EVEN number of values

Sort first: 3, 5, 8, 10. There are two middle values (5 and 8), so the median is halfway between them: (5 + 8) ÷ 2 = 6.5.

Worked Example 6: Elapsed time and saving time

Add up the parts of a trip: 5 min + 12 min + 20 min = 37 min. Out of a 60-minute hour, 60 − 37 = 23 minutes are left.

To find time saved, work out each travel time in the same unit, then subtract.

Worked Example 7: Expected results and equations

Expected: if 1 out of every 4 patients needs an X-ray, then out of 200 patients, expect 200 × 1/4 = 50.

Solving an equation: 25n + 40 = 190. Undo each step in reverse: subtract 40 (25n = 150), then divide by 25 (n = 6).
