⛵ Math Week 42: Longships & Legends: Viking Navigator's Math Lab

Ace, about a thousand years ago Norse sailors crossed the stormy North Atlantic in open wooden longships — from Norway to Iceland, on to Greenland, and all the way to Newfoundland, where archaeologists found their camp at L'Anse aux Meadows. Today you are the navigator on an imaginary replica longship, the Sea Raven, using length-to-width ratios, oars and crew shifts, distance–speed–time, average speed, compass bearings, sail area, map coordinates, the mean from a cargo table, fractions of a crew, percent of a voyage from a bar graph, timeline arithmetic, a two-step equation, the volume of a sea chest in litres, GST on museum tickets, and rounding.

Part 1: Learn the Moves

The 4-Step Problem Solver (your Viking navigator's checklist):
  1. Read: What is the question actually asking? Underline the numbers and units (m, km, km/h, hours, days, °, m², cm³, L, kg, years, $, %).
  2. Plan: Do the units match? Change metres to centimetres or hours to minutes before you answer.
  3. Solve: Do one step at a time and write each step down.
  4. Check: Is my answer reasonable? A longship might sail at 12 km/h, but not 1 200 km/h!

Worked Example 1: Comparing with a ratio

To find how many times longer a ship is than it is wide, divide length ÷ width.

A boat 18 m long and 4 m wide: 18 ÷ 4 = 4.5, so the length is 4.5 times the width (ratio 4.5 : 1).

Worked Example 2: Distance, speed and time

Time = distance ÷ speed and speed = distance ÷ time. Keep the units matching (km with km/h, hours with hours).

600 km at 12 km/h: 600 ÷ 12 = 50 hours. And 2 days = 48 hours, so 960 km in 2 days is 960 ÷ 48 = 20 km/h.

Worked Example 3: Compass bearings

Bearings are measured clockwise from north: north 0°, east 90°, south 180°, west 270°. Turning right (clockwise) adds degrees; turning left (counterclockwise) subtracts.

Heading 90° (east), turn left 30°: 90 − 30 = 60°. (If you go below 0°, add 360°.)

Worked Example 4: Distance on a coordinate grid

If two points have the same y-coordinate, the distance between them is the difference of the x-coordinates. Then multiply by the map scale.

(−3, 2) to (5, 2): 5 − (−3) = 8 squares. At 20 km per square: 8 × 20 = 160 km.

Worked Example 5: Fraction of a group

To find a/b of a number, divide by the denominator, then multiply by the numerator.

3/4 of 40 rowers: 40 ÷ 4 = 10, then 10 × 3 = 30. The rest is 40 − 30 = 10.

Worked Example 6: Two-step equations

Undo the steps in reverse order: first undo adding/subtracting, then undo multiplying.

4n + 9 = 57 → 4n = 48 → n = 12. Check: 4 × 12 + 9 = 57 ✔

Worked Example 7: Volume and litres

Volume of a rectangular prism = length × width × height. Remember 1 000 cm³ = 1 L.

50 cm × 20 cm × 30 cm = 30 000 cm³ = 30 000 ÷ 1 000 = 30 L.

Worked Example 8: GST and money

Find the subtotal first, then multiply by 1.05 to add 5% GST. Round money to the nearest cent.

$12.40 + $8.60 = $21.00. 21.00 × 1.05 = $22.05.
