Longships & Legends: The Vikings Reach North America

Picture a Viking. Did you imagine a shouting warrior in a helmet with two big horns? That picture is a myth. Archaeologists have never found a horned helmet worn by Viking warriors. The best-preserved Viking helmet, found at Gjermundbu in Norway, is a simple rounded iron cap. The horned look was popularized in the 1800s, when costume designers dressed singers that way for a famous German opera.

The real Vikings, or Norse people, lived in Scandinavia — today's Norway, Sweden and Denmark — during the Viking Age, which historians usually date from about 793 to 1066 CE. Some did raid monasteries and towns, and those attacks were frightening and violent. But most Norse people spent most of their lives as farmers, fishers, craftspeople and traders. They were also a seafaring people who traded furs, walrus ivory and iron goods across a network that stretched from Ireland to Russia and beyond.

Their secret weapon was the longship. Builders overlapped thin oak planks and fastened them with iron rivets, making a hull that could flex in big waves instead of cracking. A long, strong keel ran along the bottom like a backbone, helping the ship sail straight and fast. Because longships sat so shallow in the water, crews could row them up rivers or pull them right onto a beach. With no compass, navigators read the Sun's height, the stars, ocean swells, the flight of seabirds, and the shapes of coastlines. Some historians think they may also have used "sunstones," crystals that can help find the Sun on a cloudy day, though scientists are still testing that idea.

Sailing west, the Norse settled Iceland in the 870s and Greenland in the 980s. Their adventures were later written down in Iceland as sagas — long prose stories about families, voyages and feuds. Two sagas tell how Leif Erikson sailed even farther west around the year 1000 and reached a land he called Vinland. For centuries, many people wondered whether these were just legends.

Then, in 1960, the Norwegian explorer Helge Ingstad and his wife, archaeologist Anne Stine Ingstad, followed clues to the northern tip of Newfoundland. A local man, George Decker, showed them grassy bumps near the shore at a place called L'Anse aux Meadows. Excavations uncovered the remains of eight buildings made of wood and sod, plus artefacts such as iron boat rivets, a bronze cloak pin, and a stone spindle whorl used for spinning wool. It was the first proven Norse settlement in the Americas. In 1978 it became one of the first UNESCO World Heritage Sites.

But exactly when were the Norse there? In 2021, scientists studied wooden pieces cut with metal blades. A huge solar storm in the year 993 left a chemical "marker" in the growth rings of trees all over the world. By counting rings outward from that marker to the bark edge, researchers proved the trees were cut down in 1021 CE — the first exact year we know Europeans were in the Americas.

The Norse were not discovering an empty land. Indigenous peoples had lived in Newfoundland and Labrador for thousands of years. The sagas describe trading and also fights in an encounter with local people, whom the Norse called "Skrælings" — a Norse word, not the name those peoples used for themselves. The Norse camp lasted only a short time, perhaps a few years. Today, L'Anse aux Meadows reminds us to test the stories we hear: some legends turn out to rest on real evidence, and some popular "facts," like horned helmets, do not.

Model answer: The author means we should check stories against evidence before deciding whether they are true. For example, the Norse sagas said Leif Erikson reached a land called Vinland around the year 1000, and many people thought that was only a legend. Then the Ingstads found buildings and Norse artefacts like iron rivets and a bronze cloak pin at L'Anse aux Meadows, and tree-ring dating proved wood was cut there in 1021 CE. On the other hand, the popular picture of Vikings in horned helmets did not pass the test, because no horned helmet has ever been found and the best Viking helmet is a plain rounded cap.
