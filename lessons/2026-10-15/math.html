🛶 Math Week 17: First Nations of BC: Land, Canoes & Salmon Lab

Ace, for thousands of years the First Nations of British Columbia have travelled the coast and rivers by cedar canoe, built longhouses, followed the salmon runs, and passed knowledge from elders to children. Many communities still count salmon, map their traditional territories, and plan travel by water today. Today you'll use math the way a canoe navigator, a fisheries counter, or a land planner would. You'll level up with map scales (cm → km), distance-speed-time in km/h, ratios, percents, mean and median, elapsed years, longhouse floor areas in m², metric conversions, integers, and reading a salmon-count bar graph.

Part 1: Learn the Moves

The 4-Step Problem Solver (your canoe navigator's checklist):
  1. Read: What is the question actually asking? Underline the numbers and units.
  2. Plan: Which operations do I need, and in what order?
  3. Solve: Do one step at a time and write each step down.
  4. Check: Is my answer reasonable? Did I answer the question that was asked, in the right unit?

Worked Example 1: Map scale (cm → km)

A map scale of 1 cm : 5 km means every 1 cm on the map stands for 5 km of real land or water.

Two points 6.4 cm apart on the map are 6.4 × 5 = 32 km apart in real life.

Going backwards: a real distance of 45 km on that map is 45 ÷ 5 = 9 cm.

Worked Example 2: Distance, speed, and time

Distance = speed × time. Speed = distance ÷ time. Time = distance ÷ speed.

A canoe paddled at 6 km/h for 3 h 30 min (3.5 h) travels 6 × 3.5 = 21 km.

Watch the minutes! 30 min = 0.5 h, 15 min = 0.25 h, 20 min = 1/3 h, 40 min = 2/3 h.

Worked Example 3: Ratios and percents

If the ratio of pink salmon to chum salmon is 3 : 2, there are 3 + 2 = 5 "parts." In 500 fish, one part = 500 ÷ 5 = 100, so pink = 3 × 100 = 300.

Percent change = (new − old) ÷ old × 100. From 800 fish to 1000 fish: 200 ÷ 800 × 100 = 25% increase.

Worked Example 4: Mean and median

Mean = add all values, then divide by how many there are. For 4, 7, 10: (4 + 7 + 10) ÷ 3 = 7.

Median = the middle value after sorting. With an even number of values, average the two middle ones: 3, 5, 9, 11 → (5 + 9) ÷ 2 = 7.

Worked Example 5: Area of a longhouse floor (m²)

Area of a rectangle = length × width. A floor 15 m long and 10 m wide = 15 × 10 = 150 m².

If a 3 m × 2 m fire area in the centre stays clear, the remaining floor is 150 − 6 = 144 m².
