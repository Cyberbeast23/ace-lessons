🍄 Math Week 60: The Hidden Kingdom! Mycologist's Math Lab

Ace, fungi are not plants and not animals. They are their own kingdom! The mushroom you see is only the "fruit." The real fungus is a web of thin threads called mycelium hiding in the soil and wood, sometimes spreading for kilometres. Fungi rot fallen logs, raise bread, make medicines and even pass sugar between trees. Today you're a mycologist (a scientist who studies fungi). You'll use hectares and square kilometres, very large numbers, rates and unit conversions, doubling, percent increase, the circumference of a circle, ratios, the mean, the probability that something does not happen, magnification, percent of an amount, two-step equations and unit prices.

Part 1: Learn the Moves

The 4-Step Problem Solver (a mycologist's checklist):
  1. Read: What is the question actually asking? Underline the numbers and units (mm, cm, m, km, hectares, g, kg, minutes, hours, $).
  2. Plan: Do the units match? Change hours to minutes, kilograms to grams or hectares to square metres before you calculate.
  3. Solve: Do one step at a time and write each step down.
  4. Check: Is my answer reasonable? A mushroom cap isn't 5 m wide, and a thread of mycelium is thinner than a hair!

Worked Example 1: Hectares and square kilometres

1 hectare (ha) = 100 m × 100 m = 10 000 m², and 1 km² = 1 000 m × 1 000 m = 1 000 000 m². So 100 ha = 1 000 000 m² = 1 km². Shortcut: hectares ÷ 100 = km².

Worked Example 2: Rates over time

Something happens 30 000 times a second. In one minute (60 s), that's 30 000 × 60 = 1 800 000 = 1.8 million times.

Worked Example 3: Changing units in a rate

Growing 3 mm per hour for 2 days: 2 days = 48 h, so 3 × 48 = 144 mm. Since 10 mm = 1 cm, that's 14.4 cm.

Worked Example 4: Doubling

If a group doubles every 30 minutes, after 2 hours it has doubled 120 ÷ 30 = 4 times: × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = × 16.

Worked Example 5: Percent increase

From 200 kg up to 250 kg: the increase is 50 kg. Divide by the starting amount: 50 ÷ 200 = 0.25 = 25% increase.

Worked Example 6: Circumference of a circle

The distance around a circle is C = π × d (diameter). With π ≈ 3.14, a circle 10 m across has C ≈ 3.14 × 10 = 31.4 m.

Worked Example 7: Ratios that scale up

A recipe uses 5 g of salt for every 400 g of flour. With 1 200 g of flour (3 times as much), use 3 × 5 = 15 g of salt.

Worked Example 8: The mean (average)

Add all the values, then divide by how many there are. The mean of 2.4, 3.1 and 3.8 is 9.3 ÷ 3 = 3.1.

Worked Example 9: Probability that something does NOT happen

A bag has 2 red and 8 blue cubes. Pick one, keep it out, pick again. P(neither is red) = 8/10 × 7/9 = 56/90 = 28/45. Count the cubes that are not red, and remember the second pick has one fewer cube!

Worked Example 10: Magnification

A microscope at 100× makes things look 100 times bigger. A cell 0.02 mm wide looks 0.02 × 100 = 2 mm wide.

Worked Example 11: Percent, then share

8% of 300 g is 0.08 × 300 = 24 g. Shared equally by 6 friends, each gets 24 ÷ 6 = 4 g.

Worked Example 12: Two-step equations

"40 plus 2.5 times a number is 100" means 40 + 2.5n = 100. Undo in reverse order: 100 − 40 = 60, then 60 ÷ 2.5 = 24.

Worked Example 13: Unit prices

$2.50 for 10 g is the same as $2.50 × 100 = $250 per kilogram, because 1 kg = 1 000 g = 100 groups of 10 g.
