You already know 錢 (cin2, money) from gold rush day and 幾多 (gei2 do1, how many) from numbers day, so listen for them in today's words. We use Traditional Chinese characters and Jyutping with tone numbers, the Hong Kong way.

How to read Jyutping (sounds):
  • Letters show the sound. Numbers show the tone (the musical pitch): 1 high, 2 rising, 3 mid, 4 low/falling, 5 low rising, 6 low.
  • Example: 買 maai5 (buy) is tone 5, starting low and rising a little, while 賣 maai6 (sell) is tone 6, low and flat. Same sounds, different tone, and the opposite meaning! Shopkeepers in Hong Kong hear the difference instantly.
  • Listening/speaking tip: Say each word out loud three times while looking at the characters. Tap Hear model, then Record and compare!

Money tips for Ace:
  • 銀行 ngan4 hong4 = bank, literally "silver business." 銀 = silver (money was once made of silver!), 行 = a company or shop. 銀包 (ngan4 baau1, "silver bag") means wallet. Bonus word!
  • 買 maai5 = buy and 賣 maai6 = sell. A trick for the characters: 賣 has an extra 士 on top of 買, because you need something extra on hand to sell!
  • 平 peng4 = cheap and 貴 gwai3 = expensive. 好平 = very cheap, 好貴 = very expensive, just like 好大 and 好高 that you already know. (In formal words like 公平 gung1 ping4, "fair," 平 is read ping4.)
  • 幾多錢 gei2 do1 cin2 = how much money? Put it after the thing: 呢個幾多錢？ = How much is this one?
  • 儲錢 cyu5 cin2 = save money (儲 = store up), and 找錢 zaau2 cin2 = give change back to a customer.
