🐝 Math Week 16: Honeybees, Hives & Pollination Lab

Ace, honeybees are tiny mathematicians of the garden. Their honeycomb is built from perfect hexagons, colonies run on careful ratios, and farmers track unit rates of flowers visited and litres of honey stored. Today you'll think like a future scientist of pollination. You'll level up with hexagon area and perimeter, worker:drone:queen ratios, colony-loss percents, flowers-per-hour unit rates, honey volume in litres and millilitres, multi-step word problems, and reading a honey-harvest bar graph.

Part 1: Learn the Moves

The 4-Step Problem Solver (your beekeeper's checklist):
  1. Read: What is the question actually asking? Underline the numbers and units.
  2. Plan: Which operations do I need, and in what order?
  3. Solve: Do one step at a time and write each step down.
  4. Check: Is my answer reasonable? Did I answer the question that was asked, in the right unit?

Worked Example 1: Hexagon perimeter and area

A regular hexagon has 6 equal sides. Perimeter = 6 × side length.

A honeycomb cell with side 4 mm has perimeter 6 × 4 = 24 mm.

Area of a regular hexagon ≈ (3√3 / 2) × side². Using √3 ≈ 1.732: for side 4 mm, area ≈ (3 × 1.732 / 2) × 16 ≈ 2.598 × 16 ≈ 41.6 mm².

Shortcut often used in Grade 6–7: area = (3√3 / 2) s², or think of a hexagon as 6 equilateral triangles.

Worked Example 2: Colony ratios

A summer hive might have about 50 000 workers, 500 drones, and 1 queen.

Worker : drone = 50 000 : 500 = 100 : 1 (divide both by 500).

If 4 out of every 5 foraging trips succeed: 4 ÷ 5 × 100 = 80%.

Worked Example 3: Unit rates (flowers per hour)

Unit rate = amount ÷ time. A bee visits 150 flowers in 30 minutes.

30 minutes = 0.5 hour, so rate = 150 ÷ 0.5 = 300 flowers per hour. Or: 150 ÷ 30 = 5 flowers per minute, then 5 × 60 = 300 per hour.

Worked Example 4: Honey volume (L and mL)

1 litre (L) = 1000 millilitres (mL). A jar holds 750 mL = 0.75 L.

If a hive yields 18 L and Ace bottles it in 0.5 L jars: 18 ÷ 0.5 = 36 jars.

Worked Example 5: Percent colony loss

Percent change = (new − old) ÷ old × 100. A colony of 40 000 bees falls to 28 000 over winter.

Loss = 12 000; percent loss = 12 000 ÷ 40 000 × 100 = 30% loss.
