🌊 Math Week 6: Oceans & Deep Sea Math Lab

Dive in with me, Ace! Today you'll use fractions, ratios, percentages, BEDMAS, mean/median/range, unit rates, area/volume, scale, and elapsed time — just like a marine scientist measuring depths, ship speeds, tides, and sonar ranges in Canada's oceans.

Part 1: Learn the Moves

The 4-Step Problem Solver (your dive check-up):
  1. Read: What is the question actually asking? Underline the numbers and units.
  2. Plan: Which operations do I need, and in what order?
  3. Solve: Do one step at a time and write each step down.
  4. Check: Is my answer reasonable? Did I answer the question that was asked?

Worked Example 1: Submersible scale model

A museum model of a deep-sea submersible is built at a scale of 1 : 250. The model is 3.2 m long. How long was the real submersible in metres?

Step 1: Real length = model × scale factor = 3.2 × 250 = 800 m.

Check: 800 ÷ 250 = 3.2 ✔

Worked Example 2: Fractions of plankton samples

A research ship collected 64 water samples. Scientists analysed 5/8 of them. How many samples were analysed?

Step 1: 64 ÷ 8 = 8; 8 × 5 = 40 samples.

Worked Example 3: Percent of divers

Out of 90 divers on a coastal survey, 40% reached the sunlit zone. How many divers reached the sunlit zone?

Step 1: 40% = 0.40.

Step 2: 0.40 × 90 = 36 divers.

Worked Example 4: Mean, median, and range

Five Pacific tide heights (metres): 2, 6, 4, 3, and 5. Find the mean, median, and range.

Mean = (2 + 6 + 4 + 3 + 5) ÷ 5 = 20 ÷ 5 = 4 m.

Ordered: 2, 3, 4, 5, 6. Median = 4 m.

Range = 6 − 2 = 4 m.

Worked Example 5: Volume, conversion, and BEDMAS

Convert 3600 mm of sonar cable to metres: 3600 ÷ 1000 = 3.6 m.

A buoyancy tank is 50 cm × 20 cm × 10 cm. Volume = 50 × 20 × 10 = 10 000 cm³.

BEDMAS: (72 − 24) ÷ 8 × 2² + 7 → 48 ÷ 8 = 6; 2² = 4; 6 × 4 = 24; 24 + 7 = 31.
