Part 1: The Lesson

The six parts of a myth-vs-science explainer 📜🌌

An explanatory essay teaches. A myth-vs-science version teaches twice: once through a story people told long ago, and once through careful evidence.

1. Hook + title: grab attention with a vivid night-sky image or a surprising question
2. Myth retelling: tell one traditional explanation fairly and respectfully (not as a joke)
3. Science explanation: solar wind, magnetosphere, ionosphere, glowing gases — clear cause-and-effect
4. Side-by-side compare: what is similar (wonder, looking up) and what is different (evidence vs story)
5. Why it matters: Canada viewing, Earth's protective shield, or respect for culture + curiosity
6. Closing takeaway: one memorable sentence that leaves the reader thinking

Five moves of a great myth-vs-science writer

  • Respect the myth: retell it as something meaningful to people, not as "wrong and silly."
  • Be precise with science: name the solar wind, magnetosphere and ionosphere instead of only saying "magic energy."
  • Use signal words: according to the story…, scientists explain…, both…, however…, in other words…
  • Show cause and effect: particles hit gases → gases release light.
  • End with wonder + clarity: leave readers amazed and informed.

Explainer's rule: after reading your essay, a classmate should understand one myth, the science chain, and why both matter.

Model Essay (short version): "When Spirits Dance — and Atoms Glow"

When Spirits Dance — and Atoms Glow
On a Yukon night so cold your breath freezes, green curtains ripple above the spruce trees. Long ago, some northern storytellers said the lights were spirits dancing. That story made people look up with respect.
Scientists explain a different chain. The Sun flings out a solar wind of plasma from its corona. Earth's magnetosphere steers many particles toward the poles, where they excite oxygen and nitrogen in the ionosphere. The gases release luminescence — green, red, purple.
Both the myth and the science begin with wonder. The difference is evidence: satellites and magnetometers measure geomagnetic storms, while stories carry culture and meaning.
Canada sits under the auroral oval, so we get a front-row seat. Knowing the science helps us value Earth's magnetic shield; knowing the stories helps us listen with respect. Look up — the sky can hold both.

Notice: the title hooks; the myth is retold kindly; the science uses real terms in order; the compare paragraph uses "both" and "difference"; the closing leaves a takeaway.
