🌌 Math Week 41: Sky Dancers: Aurora Engineer's Math Lab

Ace, auroras are sky engineering! Charged particles from the Sun race toward Earth, Earth's magnetic shield steers them, and gases high above Canada glow green and purple. Today you are a junior aurora engineer at the imaginary Northern Lights Observatory in Yellowknife, using solar-wind speed conversions, travel times from the Sun, percentages of aurora nights by latitude, scale models of Earth and the magnetosphere, the mean from a table, rectangle area, cylinder volume, ratios, two-step equations, unit rates, percent increase from a bar graph, GST on a northern-lights tour, elapsed flight time, and rounding.

Part 1: Learn the Moves

The 4-Step Problem Solver (your fossil detective's checklist):
  1. Read: What is the question actually asking? Underline the numbers and units (km/s, km/h, km, m, m², cm³, days, hours, minutes, $, %).
  2. Plan: Do the units match? Change metres to centimetres or hours to minutes before you answer.
  3. Solve: Do one step at a time and write each step down.
  4. Check: Is my answer reasonable? Solar wind might travel at 400 km/s, but not 400 km/h!

Worked Example 1: Speed unit conversion

Solar wind is often measured in kilometres per second. To change km/s into km/h, multiply by 3 600 (the number of seconds in an hour).

400 km/s × 3 600 = 1 440 000 km/h.

Worked Example 2: Distance–speed–time

Time = distance ÷ speed. Keep the units matching.

150 000 000 km ÷ 400 km/s = 375 000 s. Then 375 000 ÷ 86 400 ≈ 4.3 days (nearest tenth).

Worked Example 3: Percent of a whole

Percent = (part ÷ whole) × 100.

219 aurora nights out of 365: 219 ÷ 365 × 100 = 60%.

Worked Example 4: Scale models of Earth

If 1 cm on a model stands for 1 000 km in real life, multiply the real distance (in km) by the scale factor 1/1000 to get centimetres.

A magnetosphere stretch of 64 000 km → 64 000 ÷ 1 000 = 64 cm on the model.

Worked Example 5: Mean from a table

The mean (average) = add all the values, then divide by how many there are.

Nights 220, 200, 240, 180: mean = (220 + 200 + 240 + 180) ÷ 4 = 210.

Worked Example 6: Volume of a cylinder

Volume = π × r × r × h. We'll use π ≈ 3.14.

Radius 2 m, height 5 m: V = 3.14 × 2 × 2 × 5 = 62.8 m³.

Worked Example 7: Ratio parts

If green and red aurora photos are in the ratio 7 : 2, think of 7 + 2 = 9 equal parts.

54 photos in the ratio 7 : 2. One part = 54 ÷ 9 = 6, so green = 42.

Worked Example 8: Percent increase

Percent increase = (new − old) ÷ old × 100. Always divide by the starting amount.

From 15 nights to 30: (30 − 15) ÷ 15 × 100 = 100% increase.

Worked Example 9: GST in British Columbia

BC charges 5% GST on many goods and tours. Multiply by 1.05 to add the tax in one step.

A $180 northern-lights tour: 180 × 1.05 = $189.
